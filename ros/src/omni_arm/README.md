# omni_arm: the SO-101 on the robot (design.md 11)

The arm robot is the v7 base with an SO-101 on a low nose bracket where the catch module was. It has the stereo pair in two pods either side of the bracket, looking 35° down at the floor. The lidar is at the deck's rear, and an open bin and the display sit on the plate behind the arm. `omni_cpp`'s `arm_driver` owns the arm's servo bus and this package's `arm_brain` plans and runs the tidy task: find small things on the floor, drive to each, pick it up, drop it in the bin.

**Why not MoveIt.** The SO-101 has 5 joints and works in a small, fixed space in front of the robot. A top-down grasp is solved exactly here (`kinematics.py`). The robot's outline doesn't move, so a few checked waypoints are all the planning it needs (`motion.py`). The hard parts are seeing the floor, servo calibration error and knowing the grasp held, and MoveIt does none of those. The node still offers `control_msgs/FollowJointTrajectory` on `/arm/follow_joint_trajectory`, so MoveIt (for tables and clutter) or a LeRobot policy can drive the same arm later.

| --- | --- |
| `kinematics.py` | The chain from `config/robot_arm.json` (generated by `simulation/v11 sil/robot_export.py`: rerun it after a sim change, a test checks), top-down IK, collision capsules against the robot's keep-out volumes, the gripper's gap |
| `feetech.py` | STS3215 protocol (PING / READ / WRITE / SYNC_READ / SYNC_WRITE), LeRobot's calibration file (the sim's harness, `sil_pick.py`, drives its bus with it; the robot runs `omni_cpp`'s `feetech`) |
| `driver.py`, `motion.py` | Joint angles in and out at 50 Hz; quintic joint moves timed by the slowest joint, planned through `high` / `ready` when a straight move isn't clear |
| `floor_vision.py` | Floor objects from the stereo depth: floor plane plus rug levels, a top-down grid, the ring of floor round each thing, `FloorMap` in odom |
| `height_map.py`, `height_map_node.py` | What the D435 adds to the lidar for Nav2 (`depth_layer:=height`, proposed; design.md 4.5): a 2.5D height map fused over the frames on the map's cells. Classes floor, step (rug, sill: drivable), low (slowed for), obstacle (3 cm to the robot's top, incl. overhangs), drop (stairs; its shadow too), unknown. Obstacles and drops go to Nav2's StaticLayer; `/speed_limit` slows the robot by how sure it is of the way ahead (unknown floor, a low thing, a silent camera), merged with the console's floor-edge zones. A catalog of small things with colour crops for an AI to name (`omni_mcp` `robot_floor_unknowns` / `robot_floor_label`) |
| `floor_obstacles.py`, `floor_obstacles_node.py` | Nav2's view of the floor (design.md 4.5): each D435 frame measured against the floor it sees (floor_vision), things 8 mm and up within 1.5 m (marked after 2 sightings) and anything over 25 mm, kept on the map's cells until the camera plainly sees floor there; `/floor_obstacles/grid` for the costmaps' StaticLayer (`depth_layer:=floor`, proposed, not the default: false marks where the floor's level steps still block goals in the sim). Skips frames while the arm is out, forgets a picked thing's spot, clears under the robot. ~60 ms a frame on the PC (Python, 5 Hz) |
| `wrist_vision.py` | The last look before a grasp: the gripper's own pixels masked, the object segmented by colour against the floor around it, back onto the mid-height plane |
| `pick.py` | `TidyTask`: look, approach, close look, hover, wrist look, descend, close, lift, drop; retries after a fresh look. With the D435 (7.5° down, design.md 4.11) the close look is taken `reach.look_back` (10 cm) short of the sweet spot and the base creeps in (CREEP). With `only` it picks one object an AI's find chose, ending `picked`, `failed`, `lost`, `too_big` or `not_found`, and reports its stages (attempted, grasped, released) |
| `color_vision.py` | The AI's `find` (ai_robot_architecture.md 5.2): floor_vision's candidates labelled with colour from a registered colour image, and with shape (square, round, long, or a guess when far). Also image-only blobs and an annotated image. `Finder` runs one find at a time for `arm_brain` |
| `arm_brain.py` | The ROS node (node name `arm_node`, topics in its docstring): planning and the tidy; it sends whole motions to `omni_cpp`'s `arm_driver` and sleeps between requests |

**How it runs.** The arm is split in two processes (design.md 4.10). A one-process Python `arm_node` did both until Nov 7,
2026; its rclpy executor cost about 0.5-0.67 of a Pi 4 core holding still, and it was deleted with the other Python twins
of C++ nodes:

| | `arm_driver` (omni_cpp, C++) | `arm_brain` (this package, node name `arm_node`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does | Owns the servo bus: SYNC_READ / SYNC_WRITE at 50 Hz, calibration, torque, `/arm/joint_states`, `/tf`, `/arm/out`, plays motions | `/arm/request`, planning (`pose`, `jog`, `stop`), the `FollowJointTrajectory` action, the tidy task |
| Topics | In `/arm/motion` (`omni_interfaces/ArmMotion`: MOVE waypoints with times, HOLD, RELAX). Out `/arm/hw` (`ArmHardware`, 5 Hz: connected, joints, loads, temperatures, volts, goal, moving) | Out `/arm/motion`, `/arm/state` (JSON, the task and who drives; the console merges it with `/arm/hw`), `/pick/cmd_vel` |
| Awake | Every cycle | On a request; while tidying, once per joint-state message. The tidy's odom, depth, wrist and joint-state subscriptions exist only then |

The brain is the only writer of `/arm/motion`. A motion goes out whole and the driver plays it (quintic segments, or straight
lines for a trajectory goal); the first waypoint is replaced by the driver's own goal so a motion that starts while another
plays has no jump. A MOVE switches the torque on. A brain that dies mid-move leaves the arm finishing it and holding. Same
parameters as before: the driver takes `port`, `calibration`, `rate`, `tf_hz`, `config`; the brain `odom_topic`, `config`, `depth_topic`.
Its cores are `omni_cpp`'s `feetech`, `arm_kin` (forward kinematics, `tool_out`) and `arm_motion`, checked against `feetech.py`,
`kinematics.py` and `motion.py` by `ros/src/omni_cpp/test` (`test_cores.py`); IK, collision checks and the task stay in Python.

**On the real arm (VERIFY each):**

1. Set the servo ids 1–6 (shoulder_pan … gripper) and calibrate them with LeRobot's tool (`lerobot-calibrate`, SO-101 follower). Pass its JSON as `calibration:=<file>`.
2. Check each joint's direction and zero against the sim's convention, with the arm at the official zero pose (upper arm up, forearm and gripper forward). A joint that reads the wrong way gets `drive_mode` 1 in that file.
3. Start with `rate` 50 and the gripper at 50% torque (`driver.GRIP_TORQUE`, the C++ driver's `GRIP_TORQUE`). Watch the servo temperatures in `/arm/hw`.
4. The e-stop cuts the arm's 12 V rail and the arm drops (no brakes). Decide on a brake-by-shorting circuit, or a stow before the e-stop (open item in design.md 11).
5. The wrist camera is a 32 × 32 mm UVC module on a printed bracket beside the fixed jaw (`arm_so101.WRIST_CAM`). Measure its pose and lens, then update the sim and re-export.

**The AI's find and pick (implemented and simulated Nov 4; no real camera yet).** `/arm/request` `find {id, color, shape,
max_range}` asks the sim for a depth frame and a colour frame. Both come from the same camera: `/stereo/left/image_color`,
or `/d435/color/image_raw` with `config:=d435` (`color_topic`). The node then answers in `/arm/state`'s `found` and
publishes `/arm/find_image`, again at 2 Hz while subscribed, for web_video_server snapshots. `tidy {only: id}` then uses
the find's floor map, so the ids agree.

- **Thresholds:** all are estimates, tuned on the sim's `colours` set (`simulation/v11 sil/studies/colour_scene.py
  --seeds 10`; stereo and D435 each run 70 queries over 10 layouts):
  - stereo: 61 right, 9 "move closer", 0 wrong
  - D435: 47 right, 23 "move closer", 0 wrong
- **"Move closer":** a shape seen from too far (under 300 px, roughly 0.85 m for a 40 mm cube with the D435) is reported
  as a guess, and its match scores under 0.5.
- **On hardware:** align the D435's depth to its colour stream (`d435.yaml`) and VERIFY the colour ranges under the
  house's lighting.

**Tests:** `python -m pytest -q ros/src/omni_arm/test` (no sim, no ROS; `test_color_vision.py` uses synthetic scenes and
`test/data/colours_stereo.npz`, a saved sim render). The sim's checks against MuJoCo and a whole tidy are in
`simulation/v11 sil/test_v11.py`.
