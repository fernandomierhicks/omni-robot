# omni_arm: the SO-101 on the robot (design.md 11)

The arm robot is the v7 base with an SO-101 on a low nose bracket where the catch module was. It has the stereo pair in two pods either side of the bracket, looking 35° down at the floor. The lidar is at the deck's rear, and an open bin and the display sit on the plate behind the arm. `omni_cpp`'s `arm_driver` owns the arm's servo bus and this package's `arm_brain` plans and runs the tidy task: find small things on the floor, drive to each, pick it up, drop it in the bin.

**Why not MoveIt.** The SO-101 has 5 joints and works in a small, fixed space in front of the robot. A top-down grasp is solved exactly here (`kinematics.py`). The robot's outline doesn't move, so a few checked waypoints are all the planning it needs (`motion.py`). The hard parts are seeing the floor, servo calibration error and knowing the grasp held, and MoveIt does none of those. The node still offers `control_msgs/FollowJointTrajectory` on `/arm/follow_joint_trajectory`, so MoveIt (for tables and clutter) or a LeRobot policy can drive the same arm later.

**How it runs.** The arm is split in two processes (design.md 4.10). A one-process Python `arm_node` did both until Nov 7,
2026; its rclpy executor cost about 0.5-0.67 of a Pi 4 core holding still, and it was deleted with the other Python twins
of C++ nodes:

| | `arm_driver` (omni_cpp, C++) | `arm_brain` (this package, node name `arm_node`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does | Owns the servo bus: SYNC_READ (position .. current) / SYNC_WRITE at 50 Hz, calibration, torque, `/arm/joint_states`, `/tf`, `/arm/out`, plays motions. At connect it writes the servo settings that differ (P 16 / D 32, max temperature, protections). Each cycle the load model (`arm_load.hpp`, below) and the strain detector; a guarded motion's strained joint fires the reflex; holding still, the hold trim | `/arm/request`, planning (`pose`, `jog`, `stop`), the `FollowJointTrajectory` action, the tidy task, the arm load calibration (`cal_load`) |
| Topics | In `/arm/motion` (`omni_interfaces/ArmMotion`: MOVE waypoints with times, HOLD, RELAX; `guard`), `/imu` (gravity's direction). Out `/arm/hw` (`ArmHardware`, 5 Hz: connected, joints, loads, temperatures, volts, goal, moving; currents, outside torques, strain, tool force, the reflex, the load model, what connect changed), `/arm/servo` (`ArmServoState`, every cycle: the telemetry and the load estimate) | Out `/arm/motion`, `/arm/state` (JSON, the task and who drives, the calibration's prompt and last result; the console merges it with `/arm/hw`), `/pick/cmd_vel` |
| Awake | Every cycle | On a request; while tidying, once per joint-state message; while calibrating, once per `/arm/servo`. The tidy's and the calibration's subscriptions exist only then |

The brain is the only writer of `/arm/motion`. A motion goes out whole and the driver plays it (quintic segments, or straight
lines for a trajectory goal); the first waypoint is replaced by the driver's own goal so a motion that starts while another
plays has no jump. A MOVE switches the torque on. A brain that dies mid-move leaves the arm finishing it and holding. Same
parameters as before: the driver takes `port`, `calibration`, `rate`, `tf_hz`, `config`; the brain `odom_topic`, `config`, `depth_topic`.
Its cores are `omni_cpp`'s `ArmDriverCore` (the bus, calibration, motion player: `arm_driver_core.hpp`), `feetech`, `arm_kin`
(forward kinematics, `tool_out`) and `arm_motion`; `arm_kin` and `arm_motion` are checked against `kinematics.py` and `motion.py`, the
bus against recorded packets and a stand-in servo bus (`ros/src/omni_cpp/test`). IK, collision checks and the task stay in Python.
**The v11 sim runs the same C++ driver** (since Nov 7; the Python `feetech.py` / `driver.py` twins were deleted): `sil_pick.py`
loads `ArmDriverCore` through `omni_cpp/sil/arm_sil.py` (a small C library built with g++ / zig c++ on first use) and feeds it
the tidy task's motions with `brain_core.MotionSync`, exactly as `arm_brain` does over `/arm/motion`.

**The load on the arm (design.md 4.5; implemented Nov 20, simulated on the detailed arm, no servo measured).** Per arm
joint the driver turns the servo's duty (Present Load, taken as the PWM duty: VERIFY), the supply voltage, the goal's
speed and acceleration (or the current, where the servos report it and that fits better) into the motor's torque, takes
off the links' gravity (their masses from `robot_arm*.json` `arm.inertials`, the official model's, or the calibration's)
and their inertia, and allows for the gearbox's friction: moving, a kinetic friction in the motion's direction; still,
a band (the STS3215's ~1:345 gearbox holds a good share of gravity by friction, so a still reading says little: an
outside torque counts only past the band). What is left is the outside torque on each joint and, through the
Jacobian, one force at the fingertip. The strain detector low-passes it (40 ms) and wants it past the joint's threshold
(still or moving) 4 cycles running. Then, for a guarded motion and a calibrated model, the reflex: the motion stops,
the strained joints give 0.05 rad with the force at 35 % torque until the next command (the tidy stops too). On the
nominal model (the datasheet's servo, wide bands) the detector reports and the reflex waits (`reflex_nominal`): in the
sim it tripped on every tidy-speed move. Joint angles in and out are true angles (encoder + the calibrated zero + the
sag past the encoder). Holding still under a guarded motion the hold trim nudges the goals sent until the encoders read
the goal (the servos' own droop, ~1 deg at P 16), frozen while an outside torque is felt. Unguarded (the calibration's
motions): the servos raw, no reflex, no trim. The model comes from **Calibrate › Arm: load and gravity**
(`omni_calib/arm_load.py`, its README item 10); the driver rereads `load_calibration` when the file changes.

**On the real arm (VERIFY each):**

1. Set the servo ids 1–6 (shoulder_pan … gripper) and calibrate them with LeRobot's tool (`lerobot-calibrate`, SO-101 follower). Pass its JSON as `calibration:=<file>`.
2. Check each joint's direction and zero against the sim's convention, with the arm at the official zero pose (upper arm up, forearm and gripper forward). A joint that reads the wrong way gets `drive_mode` 1 in that file.
3. Start with `rate` 50 and the gripper at 50% torque (`driver.GRIP_TORQUE`, the C++ driver's `GRIP_TORQUE`). Watch the servo temperatures in `/arm/hw`.
4. The e-stop cuts the arm's 12 V rail and the arm drops (no brakes). Decide on a brake-by-shorting circuit, or a stow before the e-stop (open item in design.md 11).
5. The wrist camera is a 32 × 32 mm UVC module on a printed bracket beside the fixed jaw (`arm_so101.WRIST_CAM`). Measure its pose and lens, then update the sim and re-export.
6. First connect: read `/arm/hw` `servo_notes` (the settings the driver wrote). Read back a servo's registers 21-36 after a power cycle (do the EEPROM writes stick?) and hang a known weight to check Present Load is the duty and Present Current's unit (servo_emulator.py's assumptions).
7. Run **Calibrate › Arm: load and gravity** (two weights of ~150 g and ~300 g on a string loop, the kitchen scale, calipers, a ruler, the luggage scale; ~10 min). It is applied only when it passes `omni_calib.results.LIMITS["arm_load"]`; the reflex works from then on.

**The AI's find and pick (implemented and simulated Nov 4; no real camera yet).** `/arm/request` `find {id, color, shape,
max_range}` asks the sim for a depth frame and a colour frame. Both come from the same camera: `/stereo/left/image_color`,
or `/d435/color/image_raw` with `config:=d435` (`color_topic`). The node then answers in `/arm/state`'s `found` and
publishes `/arm/find_image`, again at 2 Hz while subscribed, for web_video_server snapshots. `tidy {only: id}` then uses
the find's floor map, so the ids agree.

- **Thresholds:** all are estimates, tuned on the sim's `colours` set (`simulation/v11 sil/studies/colour_scene.py
  --seeds 10`; stereo and D435 each run 70 queries over 10 layouts):
  - stereo: 61 right, 9 "move closer", 0 wrong
  - D435: 47 right, 23 "move closer", 0 wrong
- **"Move closer":** a shape seen from too far (under 300 px, roughly 0.85 m for a 40 mm cube with the D435) is reported
  as a guess, and its match scores under 0.5.
- **On hardware:** align the D435's depth to its colour stream (`d435.yaml`) and VERIFY the colour ranges under the
  house's lighting.

**Carry, put down, stack and the Goalie's ball (implemented, simulated Nov 7; no hardware).** `/arm/request` `tidy
{keep: true, only: id}` picks and keeps (Fetch), `place {ahead | x, y, z}` puts it down, `tidy {tower: true,
max_blocks}` builds a tower. In the MuJoCo pick harness with the robot's C++ arm driver and firmware
(`simulation/v11 sil/studies/arm_carry.py`): a kept 40 mm cube put down ahead landed upright 1-5 mm from the spot on
3/3 seeds; a 3-cube tower stood 3 high on 5 of 6 seeds (design.md 3).
What the sim taught the tower (each a fix in `pick.py`): the base keeps `tower_keep` from the tower (it knocked it
over driving past); after letting go the fixed finger slides off the block before rising (straight up dragged it off);
the block is carried "high" while building (at "ready" it hid the tower's top from the depth look, which read 43 mm
for 80 mm); a block is closed across its faces (`wrist_vision.square_axis`: a square's pixel covariance has no axis),
and put down and gripped again once when held askew. **Where the block sits in the gripper is measured** (HELD_LOOK,
Nov 7): after the lift the wrist camera, which rides on the gripper, sees the block's near side; `wrist_vision.held`
gives its offset across the pads, its skew and how far it rode up the fingers, and the put-down uses them. In the
sim the cubes sat 0-6 mm off centre and rode 2.5 mm up; the measurement read within about 1 mm (synthetic images:
1 mm, 2.3 deg, 1.5 mm). The jaw's gap reads a 40 mm cube as 45.7 mm, so its centre is taken as half the block's side
from the fixed finger, and regrips follow the measured skew (unseen: the gap, `regrip_gap` 9 mm): the old 4 mm gap
rule had regripped every block. Tower, 6 seeds: 5 stood 3 high (was 4), layers 3-17 mm off (was 4-22), 72-81 s
(was 119-139 s); the miss read the tower's top at 40 mm for 81 (`arm_carry_tower_held.json`). Fetch: 1-5 mm from the
spot (was 5-10; `arm_carry_fetch_held.json`). VERIFY on the real
wrist camera with a block of each colour. Results by seed: design.md 3. `ball_track_node` follows
`simulation/v11 sil/studies/goalie.py`'s rendered-D435 runs.
