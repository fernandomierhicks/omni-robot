# omni_arm: the SO-101 on the robot (design.md 11)

The arm robot is the v7 base with an SO-101 on a low nose bracket where the catch module was. It has the stereo pair in two pods either side of the bracket, looking 35° down at the floor. The lidar is at the deck's rear, and an open bin and the display sit on the plate behind the arm. `arm_node` owns the arm's servo bus and runs the tidy task: find small things on the floor, drive to each, pick it up, drop it in the bin.

**Why not MoveIt.** The SO-101 has 5 joints and works in a small, fixed space in front of the robot. A top-down grasp is solved exactly here (`kinematics.py`). The robot's outline doesn't move, so a few checked waypoints are all the planning it needs (`motion.py`). The hard parts are seeing the floor, servo calibration error and knowing the grasp held, and MoveIt does none of those. The node still offers `control_msgs/FollowJointTrajectory` on `/arm/follow_joint_trajectory`, so MoveIt (for tables and clutter) or a LeRobot policy can drive the same arm later.

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `kinematics.py` | The chain from `config/robot_arm.json` (generated by `simulation/v11 sil/robot_export.py`: rerun it after a sim change, a test checks), top-down IK, collision capsules against the robot's keep-out volumes, the gripper's gap |
| `feetech.py`, `transport.py` | STS3215 protocol (PING / READ / WRITE / SYNC_READ / SYNC_WRITE), LeRobot's calibration file, the serial port or the sim's TCP bus |
| `driver.py`, `motion.py` | Joint angles in and out at 50 Hz; quintic joint moves timed by the slowest joint, planned through `high` / `ready` when a straight move isn't clear |
| `floor_vision.py` | Floor objects from the stereo depth: floor plane plus rug levels, a top-down grid, the ring of floor round each thing, `FloorMap` in odom |
| `wrist_vision.py` | The last look before a grasp: the gripper's own pixels masked, the object segmented by colour against the floor around it, back onto the mid-height plane |
| `pick.py` | `TidyTask`: look, approach, close look, hover, wrist look, descend, close, lift, drop; retries after a fresh look |
| `arm_node.py` | The ROS node (topics in its docstring) |

**On the real arm (VERIFY each):**

1. Set the servo ids 1–6 (shoulder_pan … gripper) and calibrate them with LeRobot's tool (`lerobot-calibrate`, SO-101 follower). Pass its JSON as `calibration:=<file>`.
2. Check each joint's direction and zero against the sim's convention, with the arm at the official zero pose (upper arm up, forearm and gripper forward). A joint that reads the wrong way gets `drive_mode` 1 in that file.
3. Start with `rate` 50 and the gripper at 50% torque (`driver.GRIP_TORQUE`). Watch the servo temperatures in `/arm/state`.
4. The e-stop cuts the arm's 12 V rail and the arm drops (no brakes). Decide on a brake-by-shorting circuit, or a stow before the e-stop (open item in design.md 11).
5. The wrist camera is a 32 × 32 mm UVC module on a printed bracket beside the fixed jaw (`arm_so101.WRIST_CAM`). Measure its pose and lens, then update the sim and re-export.

**Tests:** `python -m pytest -q ros/src/omni_arm/test` (no sim, no ROS); the sim's checks against MuJoCo and a whole tidy are in `simulation/v11 sil/test_v11.py`.
