# CAD working rules

**`cad/solidworks.md` is the main CAD reference for agents: read it first** (native SolidWorks modeling, the build123d → STEP → SolidWorks pipeline, STEP exports, purchased-part rules and printed-part design rules). Then read the active version's README before editing it. Current native SolidWorks work is in `v10 human editable/` (also read its `ASSEMBLIES.md` and `cad/solidworks.md`, the native modeling contract, path from repo root). The newer compact wheel + motor and its suspension pod are in `v11/` (build123d + STEP, assembled in SolidWorks from imported parts by `tools/sw_build.py`; not native features). The whole robot is in `v12/` (round 13: v11 round 12 copied clean, then revised there): only its generating scripts and a fresh SolidWorks assembly (`tools/sw_robot.py`); start there for the current robot, and use v11's README for the history to round 12. `v13/` (Nov 5, proposal) is v12 round 15 with the D435 moved from the nose to a pocket in the arm riser's front (`tools/d435_riser.py`); since Nov 9 it also has its own SolidWorks assembly of the whole robot (`v13/solidworks/V13_robot.SLDASM`, `tools/sw_robot.py`) and reads no file outside `cad/v13`. **`v14/` (Nov 13) is the last adopted robot: a clean copy of v13 with the 687 bearing roller wheel baselined** (only the scripts that build and check the robot, its `reference/`, STEPs; its `solidworks/V14_robot.SLDASM` is not on disk). **`v16/` (Nov 19) is the baseline CAD: a clean copy of v15's baseline design only** (Fernando: "only move the baseline design of the CAD, leave behind any intermediate proposals or unused files"; his deck / beam / towers / fins are v16's parts; start there unless told otherwise): the mid-deck robot, the deck at z 80–100 with the pods hanging under it (`tools/pod_mid.py`, `tools/chassis_mid.py`), the M3 spring plunger (`tools/plunger.py`), generated fins (`tools/fins.py`), the battery under the deck, the whole robot in `tools/world.py`; `./regen_all.sh [sw]` regenerates and checks everything (~2 h; only when asked or before a print/review), **`./quick.sh pod [sw]` regenerates just the outputs a pod change reaches, no checks (Fernando, Nov 19: minor changes must not cost 30 min; extend quick.sh for other groups rather than running regen_all)** (map: `v16/tools/README.md`). **`v15/` (Nov 14)** is where the mid deck was developed: its README keeps the review rounds, and it keeps the triangle study (`v15/studies/triangle`), the plunger study with the M4 cap try-out and the bumper strategies; do not develop there. v13 keeps the history, studies, jigs (`motor_jig.py`, `pod_jig.py`) and print-fit coupons. `DUMMY_MOTOR.md` describes the fit/motion fixture. Current geometry, choices and known CAD/sim gaps live in the relevant design.md sections; consult those for design changes, not every tooling fix.

- **Always pull files from the current version's folder** (Fernando, Nov 9; `cad/solidworks.md`, top). A `cad/vNN` reads and writes only inside itself: copy any file it needs from an older version, `components/`, `cad/other` or another project into its `reference/` and point the scripts there. No `../v10/...` or absolute paths; v14 (and v13) are the model (self-contained, checked by `sw_robot.py paths`; the one non-CAD import is the robot's `ros/src/omni_arm` kinematics).
- Preserve `v7/`: earlier simulations read its BOM and head meshes. `v9/` is the frozen source wheel/prototype; do not silently overwrite it while editing v10.
- Native SolidWorks parts need named, commented global controls, readable design features, fully constrained sketches, real arcs and native patterns/mirrors. Do not replay CSG primitives as a nominally editable tree. Preserve Fernando's example part.
- Keep nominal geometry, completed checks and physical validation distinct. A successful part export does not certify a whole assembly, strength, printability or motion. Check all affected suspension/arm poses, opposite-hand components and interfaces. Do not claim an overall pass while known gaps remain.
- For build123d-based versions, dimensions come through `dims.py`. After changes, run that version's `build.py` checks and `render.py`: zero clashes (every pair of parts, and SolidWorks interference on the whole assembly), short threads, half holes, pilots across print layers, one valid solid per print (since Nov 19 the screwdriver-access check, v16 `service.py`, is not run: Fernando checks access visually; run it only when asked); inspect wall warnings. Follow version-specific prerequisites such as `build.py --render-only` before renders. For native v10, use its documented native validation tools; do not assume the older build pipeline applies.
- Print-first: PLA/PETG/TPU. **Every printed part must fit the 256 × 256 mm bed with a 2 mm margin per side (252 mm square at its best rotation), measured on the real solid in its print orientation by the build's check** (`cad/solidworks.md` 18; v16 `print_beds.bed_fit`). No heat-set inserts. Prefer M3 thread-forming into 2.8 mm pilots with at least 6 mm engagement; 3.4 mm through holes; 3.2 mm where an M3 must sit snug, e.g. as the shaft or core of a PLA sleeve (`cad/solidworks.md` 18). Pan/flat heads only, not socket caps; shallow hex-drive M3 pan heads 6.5 × 2.5 mm. Fasteners are magenta.
- Model every purchased part's mating connector (real plug STEP) and its leads with the tightest allowed bend before packaging around it; keep about 4 mm clearance to moving parts and check it (`cad/solidworks.md` section 16).
- Update the model/export and relevant design baseline when adopted geometry changes. Keep validation outputs with the version; older assembly checks do not validate revised interfaces.
