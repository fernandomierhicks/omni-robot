# Bumps without bumper switches: sim study (Nov 9, 2026)

Generated by `studies/bump_detect.py report` from `results/bump_detect.json` (the module docstring has the method). **Simulation only**: MuJoCo contacts, explicit rollers, the AK40-10 and BNO085 models; current noise and every obstacle's stiffness and friction are estimates. Robot: `arm_d435_params()` (9.63 kg, SO-101 stowed), the C++ firmware in the loop (`arm` variant, adult profile), DRIVE_VEL_BODY, yaw held. Positive runs keep driving 1.5 s after contact unless stated.

**Decision (Fernando, Nov 9): not implemented.** No software contact sensing (it triggers on rugs and thresholds) and no rim bumper switches (too complex for the CAD). design.md 4.13.

## Findings

1. **Floor transitions are the problem, not sensor noise.** A wheel meeting a rug edge or a threshold is an external force like any bump: a 12 mm square rug edge at 1.5 m/s reads 665 N and 80 m/s² for a moment, more than a wall at 0.5 m/s. With thresholds that must stay quiet over every transition, the fused observer finds 8/113 contacts and the IMU 0/113. Hard-floor driving alone is quiet (≤ 31 N, ≤ 6.3 m/s²); current noise up to 0.3 A rms changes nothing (the sweep below).
2. **With the floor edges known** (detection paused only while crossing a mapped edge), the fused force observer (IMU + currents + wheel speeds) finds 74/74 contacts with fixed, sprung or high obstacles (walls, posts, sofa, leg, a table edge) and 89/113 overall, with 0 alarms before a contact; rigid hits in ~6 ms after ~1-3 mm, sprung ones in ~73 ms. Its floor is ~18 N held (rug driving sets it) or ~100 N of impact.
3. **The IMU alone** catches only the hard, quick hits (48/113 with edges known, 0/113 without): once the robot is pinned, measured and commanded accelerations are both zero. The currents carry the rest.
4. **Missed: light things and gentle touches.** Free objects 11/30: a shoe (0.4 kg), a play ball (all runs), a 1.5 kg toy box (5 of 6) and a 3 kg box at 0.2 m/s are pushed on (0.3-1.9 m) without a trace: sliding them takes 2-13 N, under the floor. A 10 N lean on the standing robot and most short kicks are missed too (pushes 4/9; 25 N leans found in ~0.35 s). These are exactly what a bumper switch (1-5 N) would catch: in the closed loop an ideal switch stops the shoe after 19-130 mm.
5. **Direction:** where the observer fires, the force's direction gives the contact bearing within 4° (median), 16° (p90): finer than four quadrants. Except the table edge: hit at the head, the robot tips back and the estimate reads ~180° off.
6. **A table edge at 0.42 m tips the robot.** Neither the lidar nor the D435 sees it; driving on, the head is held while the wheels push the base under: 28-34° of tilt. A stop on detection holds it to 6-23° (an ideal switch on the head 2-18°): a tilt stop and the arm-robot's height in the costmap matter more here than rim switches, which would not touch it.
7. **Stopping doesn't soften the hit.** The first impact's peak is the same with a switch, the observer or no stop (it is over in milliseconds); detection limits only what follows. The firmware's ramped stop alone runs on ~19 / 64 / 130 mm at 0.2 / 0.5 / 0.8 m/s, so a switch's few mm of travel only report the contact.

Limits: the sim's contacts, rugs and rollers are models (no tile grout, cables, deep pile, a rug that moved off the map); thresholds are 1.25 x the worst of ~100 no-contact runs, not a measured false-alarm rate; the gate uses the true pose and a perfect edge map; the contact peaks come from stiff MuJoCo contacts. On the robot the thresholds must be re-tuned on its floors.
