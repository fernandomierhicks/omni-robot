# v11: the robot's own code in the loop

v11 is v10 (the v7 robot, its sensors, the ROS 2 bridge) running the code that will run on the robot, instead of the sim's stand-ins for it:

- **The MCU firmware.** The Teensy 4.1's C++ core (`../../firmware`) runs inside the sim every 1 ms of sim time: software in the loop (SIL).
  - The sim talks to it only through what the Teensy's pins would carry: raw CAN frames to and from the four DM-2325s, BNO085 reports, DShot eRPM, the RoboClaw's and the servo's readings, the beams.
  - The Jetson side talks to it only through USB bytes.
- **The Jetson's catch code** (`../../ros/src/omni_catch`, `omni_mcu`): the colour-blob detector on camera pixels, the tracking filter on the MCU's odometry and attitude, the catch behaviour, the modes, the MCU link with time sync. It runs here in one process (`sil_game.py`) and as ROS nodes (`ros/omni.sh catch`).
- **What stays the sim's:** the world (physics, the thrower, the cameras' image formation, the parts' own loops inside the motors, ESCs and servos) and the truth used for scoring.

Numbers and decisions: [../../design.md](../../design.md) (history §30 for the catch-era text above). Everything is **proposed** and simulated, not measured.

## Results in one place

**The same 216 throws** (the 6-7 m catch game, home_floor B's grid, firmware adult, expected cameras, flat floor), played two ways (`studies/sil_catch.py`):

| | Caught | Not in place / bounced | Funnel offset median / 95% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The sim's own game (Python firmware on the true pose, perfect timing) | 93.1% ± 1.7 | 14 / 1 | 2.2 / 29.5 cm |
| **SIL: C++ firmware, the MCU's odometry, the Jetson's code** | **90.3% ± 2.0** | 16 / 5 | 2.5 / 33.0 cm |

By apex: 0.75 m 83 → 76%, 1.0 m 96 → 89%, 1.5 m 93 → 96%, 2.0 m 100 → 100%. Zero link errors; the time sync holds the MCU's clock to ~0.2 ms.

**Through ROS** (`omni.sh catch-score`, two runs of 24 throws at 6.5 m, return throws on): 41 of 48 caught (85% ± 5) at ≈ 1.0× real time. The returns landed a median 25 cm from the thrower's hands. Perception: 1.1 ms per stereo pair.

**The house through the firmware:** 0.5 m/s on `/cmd_vel` for 4 s drove 2.08 m, and the MCU's odometry said 2.10 m.

**Rendered images** (`studies/rendered_frames.py`, 6 throws): the same detector on MuJoCo-rendered 1280 × 800 frames finds the ball in as many frames as on the sparse ones, with its centre 0.11-0.12 px from the truth, and the filter's rim-crossing prediction agrees within a few mm. Rendering costs 2-5 ms per frame on this PC's GPU.

- **The robot crawled at 0.40 m/s in the hall under a 2.0 m/s cap.** Not MPPI: the height map's `/speed_limit` (omni_arm `height_map.speed_limit`) capped at `V_LOW` 0.4 m/s whenever any LOW cell (8 mm relief, the floor noise's level) lay within 2.5 m ahead and 0.59 m either side, as cells at a hall's wall feet always do (612 of ~700 samples: "a low thing near the path"). A LOW thing now gets the unknown floor's law, braking to V_LOW at it, on the path or within 0.3 m beside it once 0.5 m near. The hall: 46-48 s -> 28 s east, 41-43 -> 21-22 s west; MPPI's own changes (PathFollow offset 60/112, gamma 0, noise 1.0, cost weight 1) had moved it < 10%.
- **MPPI sat at the spiral room's 0.8 m gap** (5 of 5 legs; the robot's centre has ~0.3 m there, and with 0.6 m/s of sampling noise over a 2.8 s horizon nearly every rollout clipped the inscribed zone late). `time_steps` 56 -> 30 (1.5 s) got through every time and was the fastest variant on every leg (6/6 legs, 190 s against 217 for vx_std 0.3, which also got through); half the computation too. The house's 24 in door dithering is the same thing.
- **The console now creeps along Nav2's plan** after 10 s without headway toward a goal (any nav mission): 1.5 m of NavFn's path from its point nearest the robot at 0.12 m/s on the manual input (the guard still shaping it), then MPPI again; once per spot. With the old horizon it freed the spiral leg (stalled, crept 1.26 m, arrived). It needed the plan in Go To too (the node kept it only while exploring).
- **Real time with `/clock` at 4 ms** is now the arm step's default; the MCU clock's fit holds 240-290 us rms (stamps <= ~0.35 ms off; `clock_period:=0.001` for timing work).

**Why not 2 m/s:** in the hall the robot now peaks ~1.4-1.6 m/s: the height map's other limit, braking (0.8 m/s^2, est) to stop before floor the D435 hasn't seen (it sees ~1.2-1.5 m ahead from the riser). Acceleration isn't the limit (Nav2 2.0, the firmware 2.5 m/s^2, slip ~3-4).

**Square steps (Nov 26, `studies/step_climb.py`, rollers, Python base controller, no terrain assist).** The arm robot up onto a 0.8 m platform and off, 10-70 mm, at 0.15 / 0.4 / 1.0 m/s, body direction 0-180 deg by 30, edge 0 / 30 / 60 deg to the path (693 crossings, 12 min on 24 workers): **every case crosses up to 15 mm.** Driving forward (body 0) it crosses up to 60 mm at every edge angle and speed; sideways and backward nearly as well. The failures above 15 mm are the diagonals: when an edge's normal lies near a wheel's axle (body 45 deg into a square edge, or 30 deg into one 30 deg oblique), that wheel can't drive up it and climbs only on its small rollers: it bounces and the base skates along the edge (20 mm: stopped; sideways at 90 deg: across in 5.6 s). The sim's Python controller's terrain assist turns mapped square edges to a multiple of 90 deg in the body frame for this reason; **the C++ firmware has no terrain assist**, so on the ROS stack the heading node's default (forward) is what keeps the robot square to a step.

**The sim's speed (Nov 26).** The arm step ran at 0.27-0.36x real time on the home (the full stack, 40-75 min an Explore). Where it went, measured (plain timers; cProfile mis-attributes here): the bridge is one Python thread pinned at ~94% of a core with the machine 82% idle, so the stack wasn't short of CPU; the bridge alone with no robot stack ran 0.46x. Fixed, each checked against the old path:

- **MuJoCo's air** (`SimParams.air_density/viscosity`, for the catch ball's flight) puts drag on every body and the implicitfast integrator differentiates it each step: 491 vs 129 us a step. Off for the arm robot (`CoreConfig.air` "auto"; "on" reproduces the earlier runs); the arm robot drives 6.4 m 5 mm differently. Its Goalie shots now fly without drag.
- **WSL renders on the CPU** (Mesa llvmpipe; WSLg's D3D12 was slower still, ~1.2 s a frame through MuJoCo's legacy GL): a lit wrist or colour frame ~150 ms, a D435 depth frame 23 ms. The cameras now render on Windows's GPU (`sim_render.py`, started by the bridge through WSL's interop; `render:=local` keeps them in WSL): depth 5.5 ms, colour 7, wrist 6, including the transfer. The noise and its random stream stay in the bridge, so frames are the same draws; the GPU's depth differs from llvmpipe's by a median 0.7 mm (1% of pixels > 1 cm, at edges), the albedo not at all.
- **The D435's segmentation:** MuJoCo's Renderer rebuilt its segment table in Python from every scene geom each frame (70-130 ms); `sil_pick` keeps it (pixel-identical, 2 ms). The stereo occlusion in `depth_noise` is a sliding max instead of up to 64 passes (identical; the noise model 27 -> 22 ms).
- **The wrist preview** rendered at 1 Hz whether anyone looked: now only while something besides arm_brain subscribes (arm_brain asks for its frames).
- **C++ hooks** (`sim_hooks.py`, `cext/sim_hooks.cpp`, built with zig / g++ against MuJoCo's headers; `CoreConfig.cpp_hooks`, on in the bridge): the contact model's slides and ball check, the floor friction field, rolling resistance and the arm's servos in one call a step (`pre_step` 49 -> 26 us; to 1e-16 of Python's, `test_cpp_hooks_match_python`).
- **`/clock` at 1 kHz** went to ~25 readers, most of them Python nodes woken for every message (arm_brain 88% of a core idle): `clock_period:=0.004` took the full stack from 0.82x to the 1.0x cap (cx3).

What remains per sim second in WSL (sim only, no ROS): Python glue 0.26 s (lidar packets, IMU, the firmware's CAN glue, motors), physics 0.15, lidar rays 0.07, the D435 noise model ~0.11.

## Run it

- **Any variation on Windows:** `Launcher.cmd` → group 4 *Catch game on Windows*: pick the scene (house / arena), the cameras (rendered / sparse), the acquisition, the thrower distance, apex range, throws and seed, then *Play catch*. *Study: run the grid* runs the 6-7 m grid with the same options, *Study report* compares everything run so far, *Camera pictures* and *House overview* write `renders/`.
- **Watch it on Windows:** `Launcher.cmd` → *Catch: firmware + robot code* (`sil_game.py --view`: MuJoCo's window at real time, the log in the launcher).
- **On ROS:** `Launcher.cmd` → *Play catch* (with the MuJoCo view and Foxglove), or `bash ros/omni.sh catch dist:=6.5`. *Catch score (24)* writes `studies/results/ros_catch.json`.
- **The house** (`sim`, `map`, `localize`, `nav`) also runs through the firmware now (`firmware:=true`, the default): `/cmd_vel` → mode_manager → mcu_link → the firmware, and `/odom`, `/imu` come back from the MCU.
