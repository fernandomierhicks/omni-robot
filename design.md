## 1. Goal

A mostly printed indoor home robot with omnidirectional driving, lidar SLAM, navigation, RC and browser control, a face and voice, and swappable payloads. **The immediate build is the SO-101 arm robot that tidies floor objects.** The parked catch payload tracks a 74 mm foam ball, catches it and returns it to a thrower 6–7 m away; the platform stays useful for both without making the catch robot a prerequisite.

Run the robot's own code in simulation before physical validation. First real runs and flashes are attended; measurements feed back into the models.

## 4. Major findings from trades and simulations

### 4.1 Perception limits the catch (parked)

Latency, frame rate and stereo calibration stability dominate (drift 0.04° → 97% catches, 0.5° → 58%, sim). Use a stiff bar, global shutter, synchronized exposure. History §§22–23.

### 4.2 Base

**X4 with sprung pods** (Fernando, Oct 31): Kiwi caught and braked worse, rigid X4 lost catches on rugs and with a wheel 1 mm low, a rocker pitched twice as much. History §25.

**cad/v16's springs are right as drawn** (Nov 21, sim + statics, rate est). One spring per bore, two per tower, is the best arrangement tried. It rides where the sim's pod was tuned (3–4 mm above droop at 8–9.6 kg, 14 mm left to bump) and matches it on steps, tilt and acceleration. One per tower, or two in series (towers ~20 mm taller, plunger tips at the lidar plane), bottoms 3–10× as long and starts sideways ~2 m/s² slower. **Travel is set by the springs, not the stops:** the far spring is solid at +16.1 mm, and the wheel's envelope (deck opening, fins, lidar plane) is built round +15. The base alone (4 kg) sits on its droop stops, as expected. **The gap is damping:** the drawn pod has none, so it spends longer on the bump stop and pitches 3–4° more on a 30 mm step than with ζ 0.6. Plunger-guide friction or a TPU stop pad, to try on the pod jig. [Results](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/springs_v16/README.md).

### 4.3 Catch module and launcher (parked)

Pocket, chute feed and 35° tilt took static catches 80% → 99%; ceiling-aware returns reach 7 m under an 8 ft ceiling (sim).

- **Mid deck (`cad/v16`, the baseline CAD since Nov 19; developed in `cad/v15`):** a 20 mm deck at z 80–100 in four dovetailed pieces; pods hang under it and drop out downward; fins generated from a height map; battery under the deck's centre (about 10 mm off the CG, est); four service-stand pads. Wheel positions and the second floor unchanged. Open: the arm's nearest floor grasp is ~0.2 mm off the bigger bumper; the arm keep-out ring sits under the deck rim; bumper redesign not drawn. [cad/v16 README](cad/v16/README.md) (the rounds behind it: [cad/v15 README](cad/v15/README.md)).
- **Harness (Fernando, Nov 11; proposal):** leads cross the lidar plane only through slots in the side fins' shadows; zip ties through PLA slots, spare holes; 18 AWG motor leads, CAN daisy chain with both terminators on the carrier. `cad/v13` README "Wiring harness".

### 4.5 Arm

- **Load sensing (Nov 20; implemented, simulated, no servo measured; proposed for adoption).** The driver turns each STS3215's duty, supply and the goal's motion into the motor's torque, takes off the links' gravity and inertia and the gearbox's friction (kinetic moving; a band still), and calls what is left an outside torque (and, through the Jacobian, a fingertip force). A strain detector (40 ms, 4 cycles) and, settled, a deflection cue (a joint pushed > 0.7° off its goal) fire a reflex on guarded motions: stop, give 0.05 rad with the force, 35 % torque until the next command. Holding, a hold trim takes the servos' droop out in the first second. The model's numbers come from **Calibrate › Arm: load and gravity** (`omni_calib/arm_load.py`, ~10 min: two weights on a string, kitchen scale, calipers, ruler, luggage scale); the reflex is armed only once a run has passed. Tried on `arm_model="detailed"` (motor, 1:345 gearbox with friction and backlash, horn/link flex, heat, emulated servo firmware and bus) through the robot's own C++ driver: see 3.
- **What the detailed arm taught (sim, est):** the gearbox's friction holds about half of gravity, so a still reading lies anywhere in a band (still holds are taken from both sides, and a still push is seen as the joint giving, not in the duty); gravity can't scale the gains of pan (vertical axis) or roll, and known weights are what fix every gain; the soft servo (P 16) answers inertia late, so moving thresholds are ~1 N m on lift and elbow; masses trade between links and the ruler folds sag into the zeros (heights right to ~1 mm, the parts not). The emulated firmware's gain is an assumption: at 10× the step per tick, every load reading jumped ~1 N m and the fit was worse than the datasheet. First bench checks: Present Load = duty?, Present Current's unit, P's scale, EEPROM writes surviving a power cycle (7). **Touch blocks (proposal, sim, Nov 20):** two removable blocks beside the adapter, touched by the fingertip, pin pan's zero (0.1–0.5°), which the ruler can't see; the whole arm's zeros from them made reach worse on 2 of 3 seeds (wrist flex and roll poorly seen near the base), so only pan is taken: reach 4.3–6.2 → 1.8–4.5 mm (`studies/results/arm_datums.md`; findings and the CAD plan: `arm_datums_2026-11-20.md`).

- **Opening a cabinet door (Nov 21; proposal, sim, est hinge and grip).** Fernando's choices: a **low vertical bar pull** (a base door's usual pull at ~0.75 m is out of reach; the level grasp reaches 0.30–0.45 m high, the pull's middle at 0.38 m), a self-closing concealed hinge, **the arm holds and the base pulls**. `DoorTask` stands off, reaches out, creeps in with the bar centred in the open jaws, touches the door's face with the fingertips (the depth the jaws can't measure), seats, closes while the base follows the arm across, then drives the grip round the hinge turning with the door, to 90°, lets go sideways, backs off, stows. What the sim taught: the 5-joint arm can't move its tool sideways with the fingers level, so every lateral move is the base's; a level push barely loads the wrist and elbow, so contact is the arm's give along the fingers (1.2 mm), not the reflex; **the arm can't sense a steady pull** (a level pull runs ~0.1 m over the lift's axis and the gearboxes' friction holds what the motor doesn't: a locked door's 40 N read 13 N), **the drive currents can** (`W = Jᵀτ`: 16–30 N read 19–34 N), so the pull is limited on the base's reading; without it a child-locked door was yanked to ~40 N until the bar slid out of the jaws. **Finding the door (Nov 21, Fernando: an AprilTag):** a `DoorTag` is the door map's entry (the tag's id and size, the hinge and the pull measured from it with a ruler); `DoorTask` turns on the spot until the tag is seen (FIND), then averages 5 still sightings far and again from the standoff (LOOK). The riser D435 sees nothing on a door above ~0.18 m (its view's top is 1° under the horizontal), so its tag (48 mm) must sit at the door's bottom edge and is seen with the robot ≤ ~0.9 m off, at the image's top edge (marginal: 20/21 found); the proposed forward camera (4.12) finds an 80 mm tag beside the pull from 2 m (21/21). A 20 px tag gives its place to ~1 cm but **its heading only to 3–5°** (its turn barely changes its edges' lengths), so the heading and the face's distance come from **the depth of the door's face beside it** (`face_plane`), the tag's pose fitted upright (its normal level, 5 unknowns with its roll: no mirror ambiguity). Nothing about the door's forces is known or commanded: the base drives the grip along the arc at a set rate, the force is only read and limits it; the arc is the hinge and radius from the tag's map entry, corrected by the arm's radial give. Results in 3; `studies/door_open.py`, `cabinet.py`.
- SO-101 torque limits payload before tipping (0.03–0.09 kg at reach, est); floor perception, calibration and grasp confirmation dominate.
- **Proposed `depth_layer:=height`:** a fused height map so the base sees toys and shoes the default layer misses; needs the real D435.
- **Calibration (synthetic):** tag cube in the gripper, ~60 poses, floor error to p95 0.28 mm.
- **The wrist camera measures the held block** (Nov 7): offset, skew and ride-up within ~1 mm in sim; regrips follow it. VERIFY on the real camera. "In the bin" still means "released over the bin".

### 4.6 Localization, ROS and timing

- Lidar/EKF localization with slam_toolbox; AprilTags for recovery and docking (C apriltag library, decimate 2; OpenCV's AprilTag markers are the official tags turned 180°, so print official images).
- **A2M8 timing (bench, Nov 16):** exact sample clock, open-loop turn rate (12.5 Hz at PWM 660, drifting from cold), parallax-shifted angles, capsule modes deliver a turn 12–49 ms late. sllidar_ros2's late stamps roughly doubled SLAM error and took yaw p95 to 9.7° (sim).
- **The robot's own driver, `omni_cpp` `rplidar_node` (Fernando, Nov 17):** stamps every sample from the device's sample clock; on the real lidar within 0.03 ms median. SLAM in sim back to 3.7 / 8.3 cm, yaw p95 1.5°. **Bins ≈ samples a turn (`bins: 320`):** slam_toolbox scores matches over all bins, so empty bins hurt. slam_toolbox is started by `omni_bringup lifecycle_up`.
- **A2M8 range (bench test 2, Nov 17):** range noise in Stability is ~2 mm at 1.2 m, 5 mm at 2.2 m, 12 mm at 5 m and 37 mm at 7 m, 2–3× below the sim's datasheet model, with no dropouts out to 7 m; within 2.5 cm of a rough tape. Boost is slightly less noisy but drops 8–18 % of far or slanted returns, Express and Standard more. **Stability stays the mode** (test 3 to confirm). Each mode's scan is rotated against Stability's (Boost −1.5°, Express/Standard −3.1°), so base_link → laser's yaw is calibrated per mode.
- Open: calibrate the driver's `latency`, real-room scan fill (test 3), the lidar's 0° at the mount, the sim's range noise and dropouts from tests 2–3. `hardware.launch.py lidar:=true` is off by default. Details: [lidar bench](characterization/lidar/README.md).

### 4.7 Drive, supply and printing

- **AK40-10** (confirmed Nov 2 by the sim motor study): right-sized for 10–20 kg; cheaper motors overheat sooner. **Servo mode, not MIT mode** (Nov 7): MIT's range table silently scales commands if mis-set.
- **Bench (Nov 13, hardware):** the motor caps its own acceleration at ~148 rad/s²; tops out at 368 rpm on 24 V (16% under the datasheet's no-load); trails a command by ~5.5 ms, which a UART step (Nov 19) suggests is the speed loop's lag (output moves within ~1 ms), not fixed processing; its UART also reports a real winding temperature, but control stays on CAN; torque per amp ≈ 0.48 N m/A inferred (VERIFY with a hub mass); no bus voltage on CAN; the status temperature is the driver board's. The sim's AK40-10 is refit to the bench; `MAX_WHEEL_SPEED` 37.2 rad/s. The adult worst case (2 m/s diagonal while turning) is past what a nominal pack gives (est). [components/ak40-10](components/ak40-10/README.md), [wheel bench](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_bench_2026-11-13.md), [CAN bench](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/can_bench_2026-11-13.md).
- **Fork pivot level with the axle** (Nov 2): a high pivot turns drive torque into hinge load; collinear doubles start acceleration and cuts cruise power (sim).
- **Wheel noise and wobble (Nov 11–12, sim):** roller play on the axles is the click, so rollers run on 687s with no play (−10 to −18 dB est); tread tip relief reduces the row-handover thump. [Noise](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_noise_2026-11-11.md), [wobble](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_wobble_2026-11-12.md).
- **Power budget (est):** ~123 W typical, ~420 W bursts at the 21 V stop; the computer is not fed straight from the pack. Pack capacity and resistance are claims; wheel acceptance includes fit, strength, wear, grip and noise.

### 4.14 Character, feelings and voice (Nov 21–22; implemented in sim, voice under audition)

**Goal (Fernando):** Disney-level character: the robot visibly feels things, subtly, through tasks, without disrupting them; a voice far better than today's; every channel tunable 0–10; emotion easy to keep out of navigation. Details: [omni_face README](ros/src/omni_face/README.md), its [CHARACTER.md](ros/src/omni_face/CHARACTER.md).

- **Feelings are grounded, not canned** (`omni_face` `affect.py`): real events (picks and slips, the arm's reflex, lost/found, faults, battery, praise, people, games) are appraised into an emotion that fades into a slow mood; repeated failures build frustration, the success after them is pride. One feeling shows on every channel at once (`expression.py`): face (continuous eyes with spring overshoot, breathing, micro-saccades, squash-and-stretch blinks, eyes leading turns), lines spoken with that feeling, a chirp (a contour per feeling) or voiced "whoa!", and body gestures.
- **A task always wins.** Wheel gestures (`omni_console` `gestures.py`: closed, ≤ 6 cm / 14°, within the adult profile) play only resting in MANUAL with nobody at the stick, no fault, no AI task; arm gestures (`omni_arm` `gestures.py`) only with the arm free and holding nothing, each checked like any motion (one curled-in flinch was refused by the keep-outs and redesigned). During a pick or a drive the feeling shows on face and voice only.
- **Levels:** Face-tab settings `emotion.speech / sounds / face / base / arm`, 0–10 (5 = as designed; how often and how big), `emotion.on`, and **`emotion.nav`**: on, a tired or sad mood drives up to 15 % slower (never faster); off, navigation exactly as set.
- **Voice (on the main PC, Fernando: latency matters less):** `voice_server.py` with Qwen3-TTS (natural and designed voices), Chatterbox / Turbo, Pocket TTS and Kokoro, in two venvs (they pin different torch). The old voice's flaws were the processing (resampled pitch moved the formants; a 4 ms comb), fixed with WORLD (pitch, formants, intonation independent) plus pedalboard chains. **Voice picked after three audition rounds (Nov 22, Fernando):** Chatterbox Turbo speaking a designed dry machine voice (Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign), with a reference clip per feeling through a soft vocoder that keeps the intonation (`cbt_vocoder`, the default since Nov 23; Fernando: "emotion level is correct"); `cbt_voc1`–`8` (eight steps more robotic, metallic and synthesized on the same takes) were auditioned and he kept `cbt_vocoder`; they stay selectable. The emotion change is measured: excited lines swing about 3× wider in pitch, sad lines slow down. Every auditioned voice stays selectable (Face › Voice). The robot keeps its Kokoro voice as a fallback. The robot computer would reach the PC over the LAN.
- **Chat and the voice brain (Nov 23, implemented; Fernando: "the voice should be able to run commands", the handoff's defaults):** a sentence after its name that isn't a single command goes to the PC's voice service, where Claude Haiku 5.5 answers in character (the character bible, the facts now, a small memory) and can now act: a tool loop over the robot's toolkit (`omni_mcp` `toolkit.py`, **one table that also serves Claude Code through MCP**) and **skills** (restricted Python routines run in their own process: come to me, look at me, take a picture, dance in the kitchen, is anyone in the office, where's the red block, show me around, watch the door, celebrate, nod/wave). It acts as an AI client under the console's rules (lease held only while acting, AI speed caps, collision monitor, a person always wins). **One ready action starts at once; a second motion, a multi-step move or a new skill's first run waits for "go"** (enforced in code, not left to the model). **Nothing fits but feasible:** Claude Opus 5.5 writes a new skill, checked statically and dry-run in a made-up house before it is saved; the robot says the plan and asks first. "Me" = the nearest person seen. Details: [omni_face README](ros/src/omni_face/README.md) "Its voice brain", [omni_mcp README](ros/tools/omni_mcp/README.md) "Skills". The skill sandbox keeps out the ordinary ways round it but isn't hardened; safety rests on the console. In the sim (Nov 23, scripted model, single runs) its skills took a picture, checked the kitchen and came back, danced in the living room, came to the walking person, and a two-step move waited for "go" ([omni_mcp README](ros/tools/omni_mcp/README.md) Checks). **Who answers (Fernando, Nov 23: "session kept open, route all unknown things to Claude even if it's slow"):** by default a **Claude Code session left open on the PC** (`ros/tools/voice_brain.cmd`, the subscription's login, no API key): every sentence after its name that isn't a command goes into a voice desk the session follows with its Monitor; it acts through `omni_mcp server.py --voice`, where the "go" check is enforced per sentence and the AI lease is held only while acting; a reply within ~10 s is the voice's own, later ones are said through the console. Slower than an API loop and it uses the subscription's limits. The Haiku API brain (`OMNI_VOICE_BRAIN=api`, needs an API key) and talk-only chat stay selectable. The session itself has not been run yet (plumbing tested).
- **Open / VERIFY:** the speaker and its enclosure (TBD, 2); dictation on the robot (Vosk has none); real servo gestures (speed, noise); whether `emotion.nav`'s slowdown is wanted on the real robot; **the voice brain with a real model** (only a scripted model so far), invented skills on the console (listed by the voice service instead), the photo on the screen, a real wave gesture, the handshake (after the arm load calibration). Tests: `omni_face` / `omni_console` / `omni_arm` suites (sim venv), chat and `test_brain.py` in the voice venv.

## 8. Path to the physical robot

One unit before four; feed each measurement into the sim and rerun with the same robot code.

1. **Reconcile the build definition:** the sim and Nav2 to the v16 mid deck, deck/pod mounting, arm/head heights, stow and path clearance; rerun picking, stops, crossings.
2. **Compute/perception:** score routes and navigate-then-tidy on the candidate computer with the real D435 and face loads.
3. **One wheel/pod and drive:** print fits, then a wheel and pod (grip, wear, springs, crossings) on the pod jig; AK40-10 settings through the console's bring-up, current-limited. Fit the models before ordering a set.
4. **Power and rolling base:** fuse/switch/`BUS_EN`, rails; wheels up, battery, slow floor driving with arm mass; compare with the sim.
5. **Navigation, then picking:** calibrate sensors and frames, map, soak; calibrate the arm; verify stow and waypoints; navigate-then-tidy with logs.
6. **Catch later**, by explicit resumption.

Physical sessions are attended: current-limited and wheels up first, low speed before adult profiles.
