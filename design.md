## 1. Goal

A mostly printed indoor home robot with omnidirectional driving, lidar SLAM, navigation, RC and browser control, a face and voice, and swappable payloads. **The immediate build is the SO-101 arm robot for tidying floor objects.** The parked catch payload tracks a 74 mm foam ball, catches it and returns it to a thrower 6–7 m away. Keep the shared platform useful for both without making the catch robot a prerequisite for the arm.

Use the robot's own code in simulation before physical validation. Real first runs and flashes are attended; measured results must feed back into the models.

### 2.1 Software

- **Firmware:** board-free `firmware/lib/omni_fw`; COBS/CRC USB protocol 9, heartbeat, timestamped events, sync pulse. **Protocol 9 (Nov 13, flashed on the Teensy):** STATE carries each wheel motor's current (`wheel_cur`), and the motor test ramps at the rate the host asks (CMD `a_brake`, not `a_cap`, which the arm-out caps write into every request; up to 2000 rad/s², default 20); the console's Test › Motors has a Ramp setting, timed profiles (`motors_run`: steps, a speed sweep, or segments), a current chart and Save CSV; `ros/tools/wheel_capture.py` records a profile at 200 Hz. The Teensy's env `arm_bench` ignores the e-stop input and reports a fixed 24 V (neither wired on the bench; never on a robot). **Protocol 8 (Nov 13, SIL-tested, not yet flashed):** STATE reports a motor that isn't answering as `DM_NO_REPLY`, so the robot knows which wheels it has; a silent motor fault-stops only when the base needs it (the controller driving, or that wheel being turned), so a robot with motors missing, armed at rest or testing the others, tests the ones it has (hardware, Nov 13: the R4 bench arms and holds the motor test with wheels 2–4 absent; a first rule that exempted only DRIVE_WHEELS latched at once, as the motor test sends DRIVE_IDLE between pushes). The console refuses the motor test only for no MCU, other firmware or the e-stop: a person judges a bench test. The UNO R4 bench firmware now reports its three absent motors (its virtual wheels 2–4 hid them; `-e virtual` keeps them for reproduction). **Motor test (protocol 7, Nov 12):** `DRIVE_WHEELS` gives each wheel its own speed, capped at the motor's own limit (`MAX_WHEEL_SPEED`: 37.2 rad/s on the AK40-10 since the Nov 13 motor refit, 0.95 of its measured no-load speed at 24 V; 44.9, its ERPM limit, before) and ramped at 20 rad/s² (est) in the firmware; the page plots each wheel's asked and measured speed live (200 Hz `/mcu/samples`). **Bench, Nov 13 (one unloaded AK40-10, 24 V bench supply, servo mode):** full stick tracks the ramp up to ~38 rad/s, then flattens at **38.5 rad/s (368 rpm)** against the 44.9 rad/s cap: the datasheet's 435 rpm no-load isn't reached (368 ≈ its 370 rpm rated speed, and ≈ Kv 170 × 24 V / 10 at ~90 % voltage use: inference, cause not isolated). So `MAX_WHEEL_SPEED` was ~16 % high at 24 V (regenerated from the refit motor since: 4.7); speed scales with the pack's voltage (6S: 25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal), so VERIFY on the pack before relying on the top-speed margin; leaving it ramps every wheel to rest first; STATE carries each wheel's commanded and measured speed. The console's Test › Motors drives it through mode_manager `WHEELS` (wheels up). `pio run -e arm` / `-e catch`. **AK40-10 driver (Nov 7): servo mode, not MIT mode** — ERPM velocity on the motor's own loop, zero current to coast, its status broadcast for odometry. MIT's range table, if mis-set, silently scales commands (the wheeled-leg robot's AK45-10s ran 2.5× off) and the AK40-10's couldn't be checked. One CAN frame per tick round-robin. Wheel speed capped by the 60000 ERPM limit (44.9 rad/s). CubeMars-tool settings are on the bring-up list. **Bench, Nov 12 (hardware, one unloaded motor, UNO R4 stand-in):** the 500 Hz status broadcast decodes, velocity runs both ways, and 2000 ERPM for 2 s turned the output 171.3° (171.4° expected), confirming 14 pole pairs × 10 ([firmware/bench/ak40_uno_r4](firmware/bench/ak40_uno_r4/README.md)). Then the **whole chain** (unchanged firmware core on the R4's native USB, wheels 2–4 virtual; `mcu_link` + `mode_manager` on the Pi 4; TELEOP twists on `/cmd_vel/manual`): yaw ±0.5/1.0 rad/s and vx 0.15 m/s read back 1.00× in odometry, slip ≤ 0.002 m/s, DISARMED ↔ AUTO by `/set_mode` ([firmware/bench/omni_fw_uno_r4](firmware/bench/omni_fw_uno_r4/README.md)). The ERPM limit, position wrap and error clearing VERIFY.
- **ROS 2 Jazzy:** MCU link, mode manager, scan prep, EKF, slam_toolbox, Nav2/MPPI, arm, face, console, people. One implementation per node, C++ (`omni_cpp`) where it exists (Nov 7); Python cores the sim imports keep C++ parity tests; the sim's tidy runs the C++ arm driver. TELEOP sources by priority: `/cmd_vel/joy` > `/cmd_vel/manual` > `/cmd_vel`. Tunables are described ROS parameters in `robot.yaml`; geometry is generated by `robot_export.py`.
- **Arm:** `omni_arm` (kinematics, motion, floor/wrist vision, coverage, tidy/carry/place/tower); `omni_cpp` `arm_driver` owns the servo bus. No MoveIt.
- **Console (adopted Nov 7, Fernando; run on the ROS stack Nov 8, sim):** workspaces Home, Map, payload, See, Test, Robot; driving by hand and Call are modes on Home like the tasks (Nov 13, Fernando's ask; the Drive workspace is gone, page ids unchanged); features declared by the robot, so a payload needs no page edit; every button a verb the AI and voice share; one activity history; a status bar with the control ladder (e-stop > RC > stick > task > AI) and the speed cap. **The robot says what can start (Nov 13, implemented; page checked against a synthetic bench status, not yet on the Pi):** its caps and preflight make one answer (`checks.unavailable`, the status's `avail`) that the gate refuses with and the page greys every start by, with the robot's words; Home shows a health chip per check and the modes it can run first. Refused only what would fail: missing drive motors (driving by hand too), a silent IMU (everything that drives itself), no lidar scan (navigation, people), no D435 depth (picking), no MCU / wrong protocol / e-stop (all motion), a flat pack or fault stop (motion, not driving by hand: to the charger), lost localization, an unhealthy arm. `camera_snap` self-tests the lidar at boot (spins it, counts turns, stops it), so the page tells a dead lidar from a missing driver. The robot step's `arm` option (the Arm page's button) adds the SO-101's driver and arm_node once its LeRobot calibration is in the state dir. Call: camera, drive, push-to-talk, and Listen to the robot's microphone after it announces it; a self-signed https port enables the phone's microphone. **The robot computer serves it** (systemd starts the supervisor at boot; the page draws on the viewing device): on the Pi 4 bench since Nov 12 (`robot_console.sh`, no autostart there), whose `robot` step is the hardware bring-up so far (`hardware.launch.py`: MCU link, modes, console nodes); the supervisor measured 0.0–0.2 % of a Pi 4 core. The step fails as a unit: any of its nodes dying ends the launch and the supervisor starts it again (at most 3 times in 5 min, then it stays stopped and the page says so); the MCU link rides out a USB replug by itself, and the lidar/camera server (`camera_snap`, its own service) is independent of the step. Two service requirements on any robot computer (`robot_console.sh install` sets both): logind `RemoveIPC=no`, since the nodes run as a regular user outside a login session and the default deletes their DDS shared memory when the last SSH session ends; and `KillSignal=SIGINT`, so a crashed supervisor's step stops in seconds. Checked on the Pi through 4 reboots, 20 start/stop cycles and node kills ([systemd README](ros/src/omni_console/systemd/README.md)). WSL's console remains the sim's.

## 4. Major findings from trades and simulations

### 4.1 Perception limits the catch (parked)

Latency, frame rate and stereo calibration stability dominate: simulated drift 0.04° → 97% catches, 0.3° → 78%, 0.5° → 58%. Use a stiff common bar, global shutter and synchronized exposure. Live D435 trials used a larger ball and hit lighting and false-trigger limits; they did not validate tracking the 74 mm ball at 6 m. History §§22–23; `characterization/throw_track/README.md`.

### 4.2 Base

**X4 with sprung pods** (Fernando, Oct 31). Kiwi was worse at catching and braking; rigid X4 lost 16 catch points on a diagonal rug and was sensitive to a wheel 1 mm low; a rocker pitched twice as much on thresholds. Big rollers matter at sideways edges. These are older wheel results, not validation of the 140 mm wheel. `studies/base_trade.py`, history §25.

### 4.3 Catch module and launcher (parked)

Pocket, chute feed and 35° tilt took static catches from 80% to 99%; keep one-ball feeding. Ceiling-aware returns reach 7 m under an 8 ft ceiling at 35–40° / 4,500 rpm (sim). No catch hardware is needed before the arm robot.

### 4.4 Layout and CAD

- Keep the lidar forward and its layer clear; check clearances with the SO-101's real meshes (its shoulder overhangs the front edge, so no face in front of the arm).
- **CAD/sim gap:** the sim's arm base is 218.5 mm on a 37 mm riser; v10's stow pose collides. Reconcile before trusting the sim's waypoints on the assembly.
- **Pod (`cad/v11`, proposed):** wheel + AK40-10 module, motor outboard (its XT30 mates radially), fork hinged level with the axle, bed springs and hard stops. Motor bearing and spring rates VERIFY.
- **Harness (Nov 11, Fernando's calls; `cad/v13` README "Wiring harness", carried unchanged into `v14`; proposal, nothing wired):** every lead that rises to the second floor (four motor leads, the lidar's) crosses the scan plane **only through a slot past a side fin's tail, inside that fin's shadow** (0° added; the old round holes added 10.5°); no grommets, zip ties straight through PLA tie slots (55 pairs on the floor, three in each side fin), **57 M3 pilots on a 30 mm square grid on the second floor for later attachments** (each with a boss under it, 9 mm of thread; only where nothing is on or under the plate; Nov 12), spare pass-throughs in the deck and a spare left slot. Power stack (three converter modules, distribution board, Teensy carrier) at the right rear. 18 AWG motor leads; **CAN1 a daisy chain** (out and return pairs in each motor lead, a T at the plug, both 120 Ω on the carrier). Every lead routed on both floors clear of the pods (three poses), the arm's sweep, the D435's fields and the battery's way out; connectors and port positions on our boards ASSUMED.
- **Layout F (chosen Nov 3; STEP-only, `cad/v12`–`v14`; current: `v14`):** Ø450, frame chassis, pods out from below, electronics and battery drawer at the rear under the bin, face behind the bin, lidar fins as wedges in their own shadow (20.9° blind), arm/riser/bus board one module. Rules from Fernando's reviews: pilots along the print axis (else nuts or a full Ø2.9 pilot), nothing under 4 mm or sharper than ~75°, dovetails 0.1 mm over, model real connectors; **since Nov 13: no captive nuts (a nut sits on an outer face in a 5.5 AF × 2.5 hex recess), every hole through its part, countersinks 0.5 mm deeper, no dowels or special shafts (an M3 in printed PLA)** (`cad/solidworks.md` 18; exceptions need his approval). Checks: pairwise overlaps, SolidWorks interference, hole audit. Nothing printed or load-tested.

### 4.5 Arm

- SO-101 torque limits payload before tipping (0.03–0.09 kg at reach, est). Floor perception, calibration and grasp confirmation dominate.
- **Near-floor navigation (Nov 5):** the default `raw` layer sees no toy and lost a shoe. **Proposed `depth_layer:=height`:** a fused height map (floor/step/low/obstacle/drop/unknown on relief over the local floor), speed by certainty. Needs the real D435 on house floors, furniture, drops and CPU. The sim can't score pushing toys.
- **Calibration (synthetic, `omni_calib.arm_calib`):** a tag cube in the gripper at ~60 poses: floor error 7–33 mm → p95 0.28 mm keyed; sag terms matter; reach sits at the lift's limit.
- **Carry and stack (sim):** a keep-out ring round the tower, slide off before rising, carry high, close across faces, regrip when askew. **The block's place in the gripper is measured (Nov 7):** the wrist camera rides on the gripper (joint calibration cancels) and sees the block's near side; on the bottom face's plane its near edge is exact, giving the offset across the pads, the skew and how far it rode up. The centre sits half the block's side from the fixed finger (the gap reads 45.7 mm for a 40 mm cube). In the sim the cubes sat 0–6 mm off centre and rode 2.5 mm up; it reads them within ~1 mm. Regrips now follow the measured skew: the gap rule had regripped every block. Tower 4/6 → 5/6 seeds, 25–40% faster; Fetch 5–10 → 1–5 mm (3). VERIFY with the real wrist camera.
- "In the bin" still means "released over the bin".

### 4.6 Localization, ROS and timing

- Keep lidar/EKF localization; AprilTags help recovery, gaps and docking. Single-view relocalization can pick a mirror pose.
- **Real tag bench (Nov 4, Pi 4, still tag):** locks every frame on the Arducam, the D435 IR (projector off; on, it spoils tags) and colour; corner noise 6–15× below the sim's model; `omni_slam.graph`'s `sigma_px` 0.7 keeps a margin for motion. Keep `min_margin` ≥ 30. Motion blur, range sweeps and the tag family are open. [Bench](characterization/apriltag/README.md).
- **Tag detector (implemented Nov 12, software; no real camera on the robot yet):** `tag_detector` uses the C apriltag library at decimate 2 instead of OpenCV aruco (bench: 3× faster on the Pi 4, better orientation). Its detections carry the decoder margin, so `tag_slam`'s gate now acts; aruco's had none and passed as 100. WSL, synthetic: 3.8 vs 29 ms a 720p frame, smallest tag 14 vs 28 px. **OpenCV's AprilTag markers are the official tags turned 180°**, so the old aruco path rolled official tags' poses 180° about their normal; fixed. Print tags from the official images.
- **Recovery (sim):** SLAM restarts reject stale transforms; the console reaps a leftover slam_toolbox and resumes from the last localized pose on the same MCU boot (0.09 m after a kill). A saved map still assumes its start unless given a pose; composed Nav2 can hang at shutdown.

### 4.7 Drive, supply and printing

- **AK40-10 (Oct 30, confirmed Nov 2):** the model was fitted to the maker's 24 V chart (2.13 Ω effective, 0.12 N·m friction, 4.1 N·m peak; the datasheet DC model was ~3× optimistic); since Nov 13 its no-load speed, friction and speed-loop ramp come from the bench (next item; the chart fit is kept as `AK40-10-chart`, and the results in this item used it). Speed above 2 m/s pays only on long straights. At 20 kg route RMS torque exceeds rating (1.5–1.9 vs 1.3 N·m). Of cheaper motors only the 5 N·m DM-2325 comes close and overheats sooner. Right-sized for growth to 15–20 kg. `studies/drive_char.py`, `results/motor_char/`.
- **One AK40-10 with a bare wheel on the bench (Nov 13, hardware; Teensy + the robot's stack; the older plain-bore `cad/v13` wheel, 24 V supply):** at the robot's own wheel accelerations (50 rad/s²) the motor tracks the firmware's ramp as the sim does (90 % in 0.37 vs 0.36 s). Differences from the sim: **servo mode caps the motor's acceleration at ~148 rad/s²** (≈200k ERPM/s, any asked ramp; 0.7 A, so not torque: a setting, not read yet), 3× what driving asks; **top speed 368 rpm at 24 V** at 0.2 A, where the sim's fit (461 rpm) reaches the 44.9 rad/s cap; friction rises with speed (0.073 + 0.0032 |ω| A; the sim's is flat, equal near 30 rad/s); inertia J/k = 3.9–4.1 mA s²/rad, i.e. J 2.85e-3 kg m² (+51 % on the sim's) at the sim's 0.73 N m/A or the sim's 1.88e-3 at the firmware's 0.48: **torque per amp VERIFY** (a known mass on the hub). The 4 A supply never limited anything (≤ 1.4 A, no under-voltage); the robot's mass will. Wheel accelerations the robot asks (adult: ≤ ~43 rad/s² forward at the 4.26 m/s² table, ~74 with a 15 rad/s² yaw on top) were run and exceeded both ways; the torques the robot's mass needs (~1–1.5 A a wheel) were not: that needs an added inertia (~0.024 kg m² a wheel ≈ the robot's share) or a load. **Applied to the sim (Nov 13, Fernando):** catalog `AK40-10` = the chart fit with the bench's no-load speed (374.2 rpm at 24 V), friction (0.0533 N·m + 0.00232 N·m s/rad, through the model's own torque per amp) and the 148 rad/s² setpoint ramp (`DriveMotor.accel_limit`, applied in `DriveActuator` on every path); replayed, the sim now follows the bench steps (90 % in 0.122 vs 0.135 s, the reversal 0.546 vs 0.55 s, top 38.5 rad/s). `MAX_WHEEL_SPEED` regenerated 44.9 → 37.2 rad/s (the bench Teensy still runs 44.9 until reflashed). The adult worst case (2 m/s diagonal while turning 4 rad/s: ~37 rad/s a wheel) is past what the motor gives on a nominal pack (~35.6, est). Still to explore: torque per amp and inertia, the loaded torque-speed curve, behaviour on the pack, the motor's own settings, a second motor; arm-robot drive results before Nov 13 (motor study, acceleration, top speed) used the chart fit and are not rerun. [Report](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_bench_2026-11-13.md).
- **CAN timing, two AK40-10s through the baseline firmware (Nov 13, hardware; motor 1 bare, motor 2 with the wheel, 24 V):** each motor is commanded at 250 Hz (one frame a tick, round robin) and broadcasts at 500 Hz, the motor's ceiling; STATE reaches ROS at 200 Hz with no steady-state loss; bus load ≈ 42 % with four motors (every motor every tick would be ~84 %). **The motor acts ~5.5 ms after a velocity frame** (or reports late: indistinguishable from the host): the bench lags a ramp 8.6–9.0 ms, the sim 3.2–3.6 ms; the sim now has it (`SilRobot(ak_latency=0.0055)`, 0 reproduces earlier runs). Not all of the ~9 ms is the motor's: ~2 ms is our round robin and tick, ~1.2 ms the 500 Hz status (query-reply could change it), the host links more; whether the motor's 5.5 ms is fixed or its speed loop (gains are tool settings) is open (a current-step test splits it). Per-delay ownership, settings and the manual: [components/ak40-10](components/ak40-10/README.md). The 148 rad/s² cap and friction reproduce on the second motor. **Inference: torque per amp ≈ 0.48 N m/A of the reported current** (bare rotor's J/k against the datasheet's rotor inertia), making the sim's inertia right and its drive model's internal 0.73 N m/A ~1.5× optimistic on current limit and heating (VERIFY with a hub mass). **No bus voltage on CAN** (only the motor's UART has it; the robot reads the pack on the Teensy); **the status temperature is the driver board's**, not the winding's. Untried option: query-reply mode (a status per command). [Report](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/can_bench_2026-11-13.md).
- **Fork pivot level with the axle (Nov 2):** the drawn 43 mm-high pivot turns ≈0.6 of drive torque into hinge load (bouncing, wheels fighting); collinear: start 2.6 → 6.2 m/s², slope 18 → 28°, cruise power 55 → 41 W at 10 kg. Acceleration numbers are criterion-sensitive (±30%). `studies/pivot_quick.py`.
- **Power budget (Nov 11, est; `cad/v13/tools/harness.py power`):** ~123 W typical from the pack (≈45 min on 80 % of 115 Wh), ~420 W / 20 A at the 21 V stop in a traction-limited burst with the arm and computer at peak. Rails (Fernando's picks, potted modules, all in a power stack at the right rear of floor 2): **19 V 10 A buck** (22-40 V in: a 6S pack and its bursts go below 22 V before the 21 V stop, so **bench-test its dropout** before trusting it, else raise the stop or use a buck-boost), **12 V 8 A** for the arm (the riser buck is gone), **5 V 5 A**; 30 A main fuse, 7.5 A per motor. **The computer does not run straight off the pack** (BMAX: 19 V in; the pack is 25.2 V full); it sits on the battery side of the e-stop, which cuts only the motor bus and the arm.
- **Wheel clicks (Nov 11, sim; Fernando's printed wheel clicks on a hard floor):** the click is the rollers' play on their axles: knocked across the Ø3.4-on-M3 bore play as they land and leave, and sliding end to end across the 0.3 + 0.3 thrust gaps as their axles tilt. Roller count (2×5/6/8), overlap, end fillet, 85A tread and run-out each change it ≤ ~1 dB. Removing both radial and axial play gave −10 dB into the hub and −18 dB into the floor (est). **Baselined Nov 13 (Fernando; `cad/v14`, not yet printed):** roller cartridges with two 687s (kit) on a printed stepped sleeve that clicks into seats in the thrust faces and is clamped by the same M3 × 50, back to back with no axial play; the spokes' axle blocks made smaller (R8 to R6) with symmetric 45° flanks (Fernando). **Fits from Fernando's printed `coupon_687` (Nov 13): pocket Ø14.05, sleeve Ø7.15 through the bearings** (friction holds them; no snap features); a thrust land on each hub face stands 0.2 mm off its 687's static inner race, so a roller cannot walk more than 0.2 mm. The sprung pod already keeps vertical clicks out of the chassis (−24 dB against a rigid mount; a viscous damper leaks some); horizontal ones pass through the pivot. 3 vs 4 wheels changes the total ≲1 dB. A step sim did **not** show the spoke ends ("petals") hindering a climb (they help at 75°; along the axle the roller end faces stop 12 mm, petals or not); a petal chamfer is exported only for a physical A/B. `cad/v14/tools/wheel_motor.py` (prototype rounds: `cad/v13` `wheel_quiet.py`); [report](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_noise_2026-11-11.md).
- **Wheel wobble (Nov 12, sim; the hand-rolled wheel thumps a wood floor with its rollers locked):** upright, the wheel is round (< 1 µm). The rows at ±17.25 mm make a leaned wheel step 34.5 sin β at every row handover (0.3 mm at 0.5°), whatever the roller shape. Held only at its hub, it rocks ±3.9° onto a tripod (one roller, the ends of two in the other row) with 1.3 mm of hub bob: the hand test, not the robot. On the pod (roll stiffness ≥ 300 N·m/rad, est) what remains is the handover's TPU-contact stiffness change (~0.08 mm bob, 8 N rms floor ripple at 1 m/s near the hub-on-tread mode) plus any camber the fork holds (keep it ≲0.2°). **Next roller print (Fernando, Nov 12; the `cad/v14` tread):** tip relief on the treads, 0.2 mm over the last 7 mm (near TPU print precision, so possibly a wash; kept because it costs nothing) (−3 to −10 dB, 0.3–1 m/s, robust to load and roller error); row spacing, 85A, 2 × 5 or longer rollers (they don't fit) are weaker. The robot sim's hubs cannot lean, and its rollers were longer than the CAD's (22.3 vs 19.5 mm half-length) when this was found. **Since Nov 12 (Fernando) the arm robot's sim rollers are the CAD's** (`robot_export.ARM`: catalog `Printed-140-2x6-687-v13`, rows ±17.25, 19.5 mm half-length, R2 end, the 0.2 / 7 mm relief, on 687s: no pin friction; matches the CAD's sections to 1 µm). Only `wheel_model="rollers"` sees it; earlier roller studies were not rerun. `cad/v14/tools/wheel_motor.py`; [report](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/wheel_wobble_2026-11-12.md).
- Pack capacity and mass are claims; R0 and OCV are estimates. Do not carry old 7S results to 6S. Wheel acceptance includes roller clearance, fit, loaded strength, wear, grip and noise.

### 4.13 Bump sensing without switches (sim study, Nov 9; no hardware or CAD change)

- **Question (Fernando):** before drawing rim bumper switches in `cad/v13`, how well can the Teensy tell a bump from what it already reads (BNO085, the AK40-10s' current and speed, its own commands)?
- **Floor transitions dominate:** a wheel meeting a rug edge or threshold is an external force like a bump (a 12 mm rug edge at 1.5 m/s: ~665 N, 8 g for a moment). Thresholds that stay quiet over every transition find 8/113 contacts; the IMU alone 0/113. Current noise up to 0.3 A rms changes nothing.
- **With mapped floor edges** (detection paused while crossing one), a force observer (M a_IMU − wheel forces from the currents, calibrated on free driving) finds every fixed, sprung or high contact (74/74: walls, posts, sofa, leg, a table edge), rigid hits in ~6 ms after 1–3 mm, the bearing within 4° median (16° p90), no false alarms in these runs. The IMU alone finds only hard hits (48/113).
- **Missed:** light movable things (a shoe, a ball, a 1.5 kg toy box: 2–13 N to slide, under the ~18 N floor rug driving sets), 10 N leans and short kicks. A switch would catch those; its few mm of travel don't soften anything (the ramped stop runs on 19/64/130 mm at 0.2/0.5/0.8 m/s).
- **A table edge at 0.42 m** (seen by neither lidar nor D435) tips the driving robot back 28–34°; a stop on detection holds 6–23°. Rim switches wouldn't touch it.
- **Decision (Fernando, Nov 9): no bump sensing for now.** Software contact sensing is not implemented (it triggers on rugs and thresholds) and rim bumper switches are not drawn (too complex for the CAD). Limits of the study: modelled rugs and contacts, a perfect edge map, thresholds from ~100 runs. [Report](simulation/v11%20sil/studies/results/bump_detect_2026-11-09.md).

## 8. Path to the physical robot

One unit before four; feed each measurement into the sim and rerun with the same robot code.

1. **Reconcile the build definition:** wheel webs, deck/pod mounting, arm/head heights, stow and path clearance; rerun picking, stops, crossings.
2. **Compute/perception:** on the candidate computer, score routes and navigate-then-tidy in real time with camera and face loads; choose by task results.
3. **One wheel/pod and drive:** fit samples, then a wheel and pod (grip, wear, springs, crossings); an AK40-10 set up in the CubeMars tool through the console's bring-up, on a current-limited supply. Fit the models before ordering a set.
4. **Power and rolling base:** fuse/switch/`BUS_EN`, rails; wheels up, battery, then slow floor driving with arm mass; compare with the sim.
5. **Navigation, then picking:** calibrate sensors and frames, map, soak; calibrate the arm (tag cube); verify stow and waypoints; navigate-then-tidy with logs.
6. **Catch later**, by explicit resumption.

Physical sessions are attended: current-limited and wheels up first, low speed before adult profiles.
