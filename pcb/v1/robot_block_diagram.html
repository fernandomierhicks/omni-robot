Omni Robot Block Diagram

How the Teensy carrier (pcb/v1, rev C) connects to the battery, the DC/DC converters, the robot computer and the drives on the arm robot. This is the nominal design as of Nov 17, 2026. Nothing is bought or wired yet, and dashed items are proposals.

Blue sky: power down, logic in the middle

A proposal for both floors of cad/v15, at area level. The arm keeps the front and the bin keeps its place, since the arm's drop reach fixes both. Everything else moves. Outlines are at their CAD positions (1.1 px per mm, front up); pods and fins are approximate, and dashed areas are rough.

A · Power bay, first floor centre

The 126 × 112 mm space the v15 README found for the computer on the deck's top, between the pods. It holds the three converters, the main fuse, the anti-spark switch, an XT30 distribution block, the INA226 with its 50 A shunt, and the INA3221. The battery sits right below it, and the four drives' leads stay on this floor, so no battery or motor lead crosses the lidar plane any more. The spine fin bridges over the bay (only its web and flange, z 158 to 176.5).

B · Carrier in the middle of floor 2

Between the arm and the bin, connectors up, nothing above: plug and unplug from the top. Every lead is short: USB to the computer behind it, the IMU beside it, the ELRS receiver on its edge, and the CAN, I2C and pack-sense leads straight down through S. VERIFY that the arm's sweep and stow pose clear the plugs (they reach about z 230).

C · Computer under the bin

Moved from x −135 to x −70, directly under the bin, with its ports facing the carrier. The bin and its deck stay where the arm drops into them; the deck shrinks to the bin's footprint. The USB hub sits beside the computer.

S · One slot for everything that climbs

Beside the spine fin, in its shadow, between the carrier and the computer: 19 V up to the computer, 12 V to the arm, 5 V to the face and hub, and the carrier's CAN, I2C and pack-sense leads down. Today the battery lead and four motor leads climb instead.

Rear and sides

The face and the one main button at the rear. Both sides of floor 2 come free (the old power stack and the old battery tray) for payloads or storage.

What it buys

Short heavy leads, a lower CG (the converters about 0.4 kg, est, down 80 mm), the carrier central and easy to reach, and floor 2 opened up. The cost: the power bay is reached through the lidar layer between the fins, or by lifting floor 2, which suits wiring that's set once.

Answers

  • Power modules on the first floor: yes. All three converters are at most 32 mm tall, under the 48 mm clear below the lidar's band, and they sit right over the battery.
  • Carrier on the first floor: it fits but isn't recommended. It is about 30 mm tall with the CAN modules standing, but you would reach it through the lidar layer, and every lead to floor 2 would cross the scan plane.
  • The computer on the first floor instead (the README's idea): it lowers 430 g by about 110 mm, but its five USB/HDMI leads would cross the lidar plane and the power would have to stay up. Power down is the better trade.

Power

The main switch (the e-stop), the 30 A fuse and three discrete DC/DC converters (19 V, 12 V, 5 V). There is no power board. The drives take the pack directly. An INA226 measures total current, and an INA3221 measures the arm's 12 V, the computer's 19 V and the 5 V rail.

Teensy carrier

Real-time I/O: the wheel CAN bus, the IMU, RC override, pack voltage for the 21 V stop, the current monitors, the buzzer, the RGB status LED and a NeoPixel strip. Firmware core: firmware/lib/omni_fw.

Robot computer

ROS 2 and everything with bandwidth: the D435, the arm adapter, the Teensy and the lidar on its four USB-A ports, the display on HDMI, and the mic plus spares through a hub on USB-C. It serves the web console over Wi-Fi.

Not settled yet

  • The main switch's rating. It carries the whole robot (20 A bursts, 30 A fuse) and the converters' inrush at switch-on, and few latching e-stop buttons are rated for that much DC. Choose a rated battery-disconnect switch, or let the button drive a high-current anti-spark switch.
  • The main switch drops the computer without a shutdown. That's acceptable for an e-stop; decide whether everyday power-off should shut down first.
  • The robot computer. BMAX B8A Pro and GMKtec M5 Ultra lead; none is bought. The M5 Ultra has fewer USB ports and would put more on the hub.
  • Where the CAN chain's far-end 120 Ω lives (harness or the last drive). The drives' own termination must be off.
  • The 19 V converter's 22 V input floor, which needs a bench test against a sagging 6S pack.
