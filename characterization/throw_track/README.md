## What it measures, and what it can't

| Measured (one camera, Arducam IMX708 on the Pi 4) | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| Detector rate given the flight, vs range (the ball's apparent size) | `VisionConfig.drop_prob` |
| Wrong blobs per in-view frame | `false_prob` |
| Centroid noise: rms to the fitted arc, and the arc-free second differences | `edge_sigma` (through the sim's own blob model) |
| Ball size noise, fitted vs assumed size | `sigma_size_rel` / `ball_size_rel` (reported; the gate shows its effect) |
| Blob elongation → effective exposure | `exposure` (indicative: the threshold trims the streak's faint ends) |
| Sensor stamp → host, per frame | `latency_mean`, `latency_jitter` |
| The live tracker's crossing of the rim plane vs the arc's, by time to go; the score prediction vs your y / n | the catch rate's perception term; `crossing_sigma` calibration |

**Not measurable here:** stereo calibration drift (one camera), spin (Magnus), global-shutter behaviour (the IMX708 is rolling), and exposure → glass latency (needs the LED / flash rig: `../realsense/rs_flash_latency.py`, never run). The fitted arc uses the same frames as the detections, so it bounds the noise and the filter's convergence, **not** absolute bias: the basket's tag is the independent check. The field of view is a datasheet estimate until you calibrate (`--fovy`, or `camera.f_px` in session.json from `omni_calib`).

## Procedure (Fernando, ~30 min of throwing)

1. **Rig.** A basket with a ~Ø330 mm rim (the funnel's) where a robot would wait, an AprilTag (36h11, id 0, black square 100 mm; `--tag-size`) on its outer wall facing the thrower, ~0.10 m below the rim (`--tag-below-rim`). The camera beside or behind the basket looking at the thrower. Measure the tag's centre below the rim plane and the rim radius; they go in as options. Measure the throwing distance.
2. **Ball colour.** `bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh picam`, click the real ball, adjust, **save settings**, Ctrl-C. `record_throws.py` reads that saved file (the exposure and gain too: prefer a fixed 2–4 ms exposure indoors; 8 ms blurs a 10 m/s ball by 8 cm).
3. **Record.** `bash characterization/throw_track/tt.sh record --name garage --distance 4.5 --count 30`. It takes a still of the basket first (the tag), arms, and saves a throw as soon as a ball moves. Throw; it prints `throw N written`, then asks **`did it go in the basket? [y/n, s=discard, q=finish]`**. Type the answer, throw the next. Dumps take a few seconds; the next throw can already fly. 30 throws at 3 distances is a good set. Stop with `q` or Ctrl-C (it takes a second still at the end: if the tag moved, the camera or basket was bumped).
4. **Fetch and analyse** (the code does this while you keep throwing the next session):
   ```
   bash characterization/throw_track/tt.sh fetch            # latest session, Pi -> data/
   bash characterization/throw_track/tt.sh tag              # tag pose (OpenCV: the realsense venv) -> session.json
   bash characterization/throw_track/tt.sh analyze          # results/analysis_<session>.json + a summary
   python fit_vision.py results/analysis_<session>.json     # results/vision_measured.json
   python gate_sim_vs_real.py results/analysis_<session>.json
   ```
   Only one process can hold the camera: stop `bv.sh` and the camera head first.
5. **No camera?** `tt.sh synthetic` (on the Pi) or `record_throws.py --synthetic` (anywhere) renders throws with known truth, in the same format.

## How the analysis works

- **A, the live pass:** the robot's `Perception` (`omni_catch`: detector, image-space EKF) is run over each throw's frames in order, one camera at the origin, world z up (gravity found from the throws). The track start, readiness and its crossing of the rim plane at every frame are kept.
- **B, the arc:** `arcfit.fit_arc` fits a ballistic arc with the robot's drag law to the detections (centroid, apparent size, the ball's 74 mm and gravity fix the scale). Gravity's direction is a free parameter (one monocular arc fixes the camera's tilt to ~0.4°, which is 4 px at 4 m), pooled over the session's throws as a median and then a soft prior. Then the detector is re-run with the arc's prediction as its window: it finds the ball in frames the live pass missed (far away, small), which is the detector's rate on its own, and the arc is refitted on all of it.
- **C, the metrics** (see the table above). Tracker error is reported by time to go before the ball crosses the rim plane (1.0 … 0.1 s), with the Mahalanobis error (is `crossing_sigma` honest?), and the score prediction as a confusion matrix against your labels, at each time to go.
- **Monocular is hard at the start.** `ImageTracker` starts a track from a ball's size alone (range = f R / b), which jitters by tens of cm at 4 m, so a track often needs a few more frames than in stereo; the first second's crossing error is the honest cost of one camera. The robot has two.

## From measurement to sim (rule 2)

`fit_vision.py` writes the values and **where each came from**. The sim jitters the colour threshold per **pixel** (`edge_sigma`), which moves the centroid far less than that, so it runs the sim's own blob model (`catch_world.ball_pixels`, split out of `CameraSim.render` unchanged) on the fitted arcs at several `edge_sigma` and matches the measured centroid noise: on synthetic throws with 0.4 px of centroid noise it needs `edge_sigma` ≈ 2 against the sim's current 0.6 (~0.13 px of centroid noise). It also reports whether that edge jitter makes the ball's *size* noisier than the camera's (the sim's does, in the synthetic case).

`gate_sim_vs_real.py` then rebuilds each real flight with the sim's frames and runs the same analysis: centroid noise, detection rate, track start range and the crossing error by time to go, real and sim side by side. "Pass" = within 1.5× (rates within 0.05); with under 30 throws it says the differences prove little. A failure means the sim's camera model leaves something out: fix the model (blur, size noise, gaps), not the measurement. After a pass: `WorldConfig(vision=catch_world.measured_cameras())`, rerun `studies/sil_catch.py both --robot drawn`, and write the change in the catch rate into design.md 4.1.

The numbers belong to the *Arducam* (rolling, mono, 768 × 432 at 120 fps). The robot's cameras (two global-shutter cameras) will be measured the same way when they exist; until then these are the best real values, flagged as another camera's.

The night sessions above supersede the original daytime note that no real ball had been thrown.

**The verdict (`rs_predict.py`).** Reference = the drag-law arc through all detections (gravity along the mount's `up`) and where it crosses the rim plane going down; the radial miss `d` from the rim's axis gives in (`d < R - r_ball`), rim (touches) or out. Causal = the same arc refitted after each detection. **Time to correct** = the time to go at the earliest detection from which the verdict never again differs from the reference. The association is the offline fit's (an oracle), so wrong-blob rejection isn't tested here; throws whose reference touches the rim are flagged ambiguous. With the 160.7 mm test ball in the 284.48 mm opening the clean-entry window is small: the ball's centre within 62 mm of the axis is "in", 62-222 mm "rim" (it touches the edge), beyond 222 mm "out"; the binary call used for the time to correct is the centre inside the rim circle (d < 142 mm). The reference and the live estimate come from the same camera, so a wrong field of view, pitch or rim offset moves both: this tests how soon the estimate settles, not its absolute accuracy (hold a ball still at the rim's centre and edges, or watch ~10 throws by eye, to check that once).

**Mount geometry (a CAD input).** With the 30 deg CAD the rim centre is 35 cm above the camera's optical axis and 2 cm ahead of its optical plane, 87 deg off the axis: it is never in view, so the crossing is always extrapolated. For a lob released 4.5 m out (or 6 m) and 1.15 m above the camera, 1 s in the air, aimed through the rim's centre, the ball is out of the view this long before the crossing (ideal pinhole, the bucket's position as drawn, only the pitch varied; depth / IR field of view 87 x 58 deg, colour 69 x 42 deg):

| Camera pitch | blind, depth / IR (4.5 m / 6 m) | blind, colour (4.5 m / 6 m) |

| 20 deg | 406 / 266 ms | 558 / 406 ms |
| **30 deg (the CAD)** | **236 / 148 ms** | **368 / 236 ms** |

**How the balls look.** These figures use the accepted colour picks in the eight visually clear loc clips. They include a few release/hand-adjacent points; they do not sample pixels where detection failed. Diameter means **equivalent diameter of the thresholded colour mask**, with `size_gain` removed, not a caliper measurement of the complete silhouette. The colour ball is usually **darker than its immediate surroundings**: median encoded-luma difference (core minus annulus) is −0.173 / −0.070 / −0.028 for loc1/2/3. **Camera/light choices (inference and proposals, not measured comparisons).** D435 IR/depth uses a global shutter; its RGB is rolling ([D400 datasheet](https://realsenseai.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Intel-RealSense-D400-Series-Datasheet-October-2024.pdf)). Global shutter removes row-time distortion; it does not remove motion during an exposure ([RealSense motion/exposure guide](https://dev.realsenseai.com/docs/high-speed-capture-mode-of-intel-realsense-depth-camera-d435/)). Near the image centre, blur travel is approximately `f_px × transverse_relative_speed / range × exposure`; add camera angular image motion as well. At colour f≈616 px, a 2 m/s sideways base at 2 m adds ~1.23 px over 2 ms or 4.93 px over 8 ms, if perpendicular to the sight line (at 1 m, twice that). This is a calculation, not a moving-robot result. The recordings do not measure scan readout, vibration, tracking under robot rotation, or relative ball/base motion. The wider IR view is attractive; existing 1 ms dark images and failed cue selection do not tell us its best achievable performance.

Going from 8 to 2 ms needs roughly **4× the incident light** for the same photoelectron count at fixed optics; from 2 to 0.5 ms likewise. Gain amplifies noise too and cannot replace photons. Use steady broad visible LED light or daylight first, aimed over the flight volume, then test a camera-adjacent IR flood separately if needed. Measure ball contrast, clipping and detection, rather than making the whole room look bright; WB should maximize ball/background separation and stability, not minimize room saturation. Illumination from a light on the robot falls with distance squared. We did not measure lux, spectral response, photon/read noise or a lamp's flicker. A laser spot illuminates only a spot and needs to find the ball first; it is a poor acquisition plan. Prefer diffuse, eye-safe LED illumination.

Keep the large yellow/orange foam ball as a diagnostic reference, but include the **actual 74 mm robot ball** before choosing a camera. At 6 m and f=616 px, its ideal diameter is **7.6 px**, versus 16.5 px for the 160.7 mm ball; its area is only 21%. `ArcCfg.min_r_px=4` is already near that smaller ball's ideal radius, before threshold loss and coarse sampling (the configured `size_gain` changes the exact cutoff). Today's result on a large ball cannot validate 6–7 m tracking of the robot ball. A pickleball may add stereo texture, but holes remove coloured area, its size is much smaller than this test ball, and it changes drag, spin and the catcher/launcher problem. It was not tested. Visible yellow/orange does not establish near-IR reflectance. Retroreflective tape/ball deserves a separate A/B session with projector on/off, several distances and rotations; a small patch flickers with spin, saturation may spoil the depth map, and return strength depends on source/imager angle. The D435 projector is a sparse pattern designed for stereo texture ([projector guide](https://dev.realsenseai.com/docs/projectors/)), not a guaranteed floodlit reference ball. IR association can cross-check colour errors but shares this rig's pose/calibration; external landing truth is still required.

The Arducam/Camera Module 3 can help **frame rate and delivery latency**: the earlier Pi camera-head run processed 120 fps with ~15 ms sensor-stamp-to-publish and 0.45 core. That was not these thrown balls or these room/exposure conditions. Module 3 remains rolling shutter, and a single camera lacks the D435's hardware depth/stereo. Fix focus for the throw volume and compare the same ball/light/distance at fixed exposures; there is no recorded matched throw comparison proving it is brighter or more accurate. A future colour global-shutter camera is a plausible option, not a conclusion from these clips ([Raspberry Pi shutter explanation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/global-shutter-camera.html)).

**Proposed next experiments, in order.**

1. **Make a small honest benchmark before another 25 throws.** Fix the rig rigidly, check a stationary fiducial each clip, measure both balls and distances, and correct/tag exposure units and ball metadata. Record short continuous blocks or externally marked attempts irrespective of the colour trigger, including failed triggers; retain a manual/foot-button mark and an external phone view of release and rim. Score "no throw / handling / free flight / obscured / out of view" separately from in/rim/out. Keep negative clips and long idle footage. Store to faster external storage after a measured USB/capture stress check, or use colour+depth for most runs and brief four-stream side sessions; show spool capacity and an unambiguous READY indication. Wired 100 Mb/s is ~11 MB/s, insufficient for continuous ~220 MB/s four-stream raw transfer; copying after capture and smaller/compressed recordings are different problems. Do not delete originals without Fernando's agreement and verified copies.
2. **A guided setup tuner, not whole-frame Hough.** Ask for the ball in a marked image region, then a short left/right/up/down and toward/away movement that separates it from the stand. Use the common motion across a round/elliptical contour, colour, size/depth consistency and stereo/depth support to identify the object. Depth is optional/noisy, not a mandatory veto. Hough/edge fits run in the small proposed regions, at setup rate; reject ambiguous multiple candidates and allow a centre click. Optimize exposure/gain/WB jointly for ball separation in the flight volume under a short-exposure cap; test a few poses/rotations, not just one table pixel. Print expected radius/contrast, decoy count and a preview before accepting. A tune may legitimately report insufficient light. This movement identifies the ball for tuning; it must not count as a throw.
3. **Causal free-flight recognition in the real robot code.** Keep `FastDetector`/the matching C++ core as the candidate generator, and add local shape/IR/depth support and several competing tracks. Separate "ball seen" → "held/moving candidate" → "free flight confirmed" → "temporarily obscured/lost". Compare the robot's gravity+drag flight model against held/constant-velocity hypotheses in 3D with measurement uncertainty, known IMU/calibrated up and camera pose at exposure time; use genuine depth growth to catch head-on throws, not image-speed alone. Require consecutive predictive innovations and separation from the hand/foreground where observable; defer ambiguous short tracks. The present size/depth gate rejects a skin patch on an arm but cannot reject a correctly sized ball in a hand. Global background motion should inhibit a fixed-rig trigger or be compensated by measured ego-motion. Hough alone does not distinguish a carried round ball. None of this new classifier was implemented or timed in this audit. Change Python and C++ catch twins together, keep Pi imports numpy-only where required, and score the frozen before/after on **all** 28 clips plus continuous negatives. Tune only on night/loc1/loc2; run loc3 once after freezing knobs, then acquire a fresh held-out room. Measure frame recall, wrong associations, confirmation delay, reacquisition and missed attempts, not just "an arc exists".
4. **Light/shutter A/B with the camera stationary.** Same marked trajectory/ball: 0.5/1/2/4/8 ms, a fixed WB, gain sweeps and room light vs added broad LED; IR 0.5/1/2 ms with projector on/off. A small static ball grid first cheaply separates reflectance/SNR from motion. Then a handful of independently recorded throws per promising setting. Compare the D435 and Arducam sequentially in identical conditions, or simultaneously only with measured timestamps and calibrated views. Test retroreflection as its own experiment; do not mix its altered ball with foam-ball scores.
5. **Simulation and hardware-in-loop, with clear boundaries.** Replay the measured rays/ranges and gaps through the real robot tracker/controller first; simulate base translation/rotation, camera pose, FOV, exposure integration, rolling rows, vibration and delay sweeps next. Use measured uncertainty ranges; rendered sim does not validate real photometry, reflectance, flicker, Bayer/shading, stereo failure, timing bias or yellow-ball drag. A fixed real camera can measure a world flight that drives a simulated moving robot (`ghost`), with calibrated camera→world and synchronized exposure times; the simulated robot's changing pose must be used consistently. That tests compute, transport, prediction and reachability under the assumed motion model. It **does not** put real ego-motion blur/parallax in the stationary camera. Finally move the real camera on a measured slider/turntable or the robot to test those optical effects. Claim "would reach" only against an independently scored world crossing and logged actuation deadlines, not agreement between fits of the same video.
6. **Combined Pi acceptance run.** Ethernet, wall clock after clock checks, capture and real detector/tracker/behaviour on the Pi, only physics on the PC. Measure a sustained run's per-stage median/p95/p99/max exposure-to-detection/command age, queue age, dropped frames, per-core CPU and thermal throttling, with and without optional ROI shape support. Bounded latest-frame queues should expose/drop stale work. The typical replay times justify this experiment; they do not yet prove the combined tier fits, nor that Pi Nav2 now fits. Keep navigation on the proposed main computer until separately demonstrated.
