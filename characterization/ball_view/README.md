# Ball view: the robot's ball detector, live, with knobs

A web page served from the Pi that shows a camera's image with the **robot's own ball detector** drawn on it (`omni_catch.detector` and its C++ pixel loops, `fast_detector`), sliders for every detector setting, the camera's exposure, and the ball's 3D position. Nothing here is robot code: it is for tuning the colour window and seeing how well the detector holds the ball before trusting it on a throw. What you set here is what `DetectorConfig` takes (the page prints the values to paste).

**Status (Nov 1): paused** (Fernando) until the stereo global-shutter colour cameras and a mobile robot exist (design.md 4.10). The lab is stopped on the Pi. Where it was left: the gesture identification was fixed on two recorded clips (below) but not confirmed live afterwards; searching runs ~5 fps on the Pi 4. Camera-motion results are in `../motion_test/README.md`.

## Bucket ball lab (Oct 31): gesture, tuning, 3D flight, browser status

**Experimental first implementation, not a validated every-throw detector.** Fernando's three foam balls (orange, yellow, purple) use the measured orange ball's nominal **160.7 mm / 73 g**, since they are the same type; individual dimensions/masses and drag remain unmeasured. The camera stays in the bucket. Browser indicators replace the proposed GPIO LEDs.

```
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh bucket                # upload and start, port 8086
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh bucket --profile purple
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh bucket --arducam      # + Arducam witness (~56 fps, saved in every raw clip)
bash characterization/ball_view/bucket.sh status
bash characterization/ball_view/bucket.sh stop               # only this app's recorded PID
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh bucket-demo            # synthetic camera on the Pi
```

Open **http://omni-cam.local:8086/** on the iPad/PC. The old ball view remains on 8085; do not run two programs on the same camera. The new launcher does not kill other camera programs, flash firmware, erase recordings or change network settings. Deployment is additive; data lives outside the deployed code. `bucket_live.log` and the owned PID file are in `~/omni_ws/`. If the camera is absent the page still works, reports **Camera unavailable**, and retries automatically; reconnecting resets identity and requires a fresh gesture. A generic error from the old RealSense helper is not evidence of a firmware or USB2 fault: check the actual USB listing. On Oct 31 evening the D435 was intentionally disconnected (Fernando); no live D435 test was performed.

**Use it:** choose the ball colour profile (a label, not a preset colour assumption), hold the ball where it is visible and draw **two large squares** at a comfortable pace. The gesture matcher tolerates imperfect corners and variable pace; candidates are colour-independent contours from grey and individual RGB edges, screened using known size/depth where available. Candidate numbers and square counts are drawn on the video. If ambiguous, click a visible circular proposal to select it. It then tests a finite exposure/gain/WB grid while maintaining that object identity. Keep moving the ball gently. The initial exposure cap is 2 ms; increase the cap or add light if it reports insufficient light. It learns even a dim/desaturated object and supports a hue interval crossing red. After 20 successful verification frames it shows **Ready** and starts recording. Settings are saved per ball in `~/omni_ws/ball_view_settings/bucket_<colour>.json`; save/load and filter/exposure controls remain available. Changes to material camera/filter/ball settings withdraw Ready until reverified. A lost target requires Identify again; it never deliberately hands the identity to the room's largest coloured blob.

**Gesture proposals (Nov 1 fix, `bucket_vision.BlobProposer`).** Fernando's first real clip (the orange ball moved slowly at ~5.3 m, ~9 px radius, held from below against a picture frame) showed the contour proposer finding the ball within 12 px in 23 of 50 analysed frames, at radius 4–5 px and 5–10 px off; the small radius then put it at 9–12 m, so the depth check dropped it or left it unconfirmed, and no square could complete. Its edges merged with the arm's and the frame's into non-round contours. The replacement is still colour-independent: a slow background model (Lab, half resolution, noise-scaled threshold: gain-128 noise had lit 5% of pixels) picks the regions that moved (plus a window round the ball being tuned); inside them, centre-vs-ring blob scores at each pyramid scale (a uniform centre unlike its ring; edges and texture score low), moving peaks first, each refined by a local mask (ball against its ring, the arm opened away) for the centre and radius. On that clip: **48 of 50 frames within 6 px (the first has no background), radius 9.5 px, depth-confirmed in 28**; the test `test_far_held_ball_is_proposed_in_real_frames` keeps 15 of its frames (`test_data/nov1_far_ball`). Pi 4: 84 ms per frame for the proposer alone (old 23 ms); live searching 134 ms per analysed frame, ~7.5 fps, about what it was. A ball that never moves is not proposed while searching (click it instead: the click uses every spot plus the old contours). Not yet tried: a square gesture with the new proposer, the yellow and purple balls, a busy moving background.

**Gesture and tuning fixes (Nov 1, after Fernando's first live identification).** The tuner ran a fixed grid from 500 µs / gain 64 upward (1/8 of the working brightness) with a score that favoured short exposures: the image went nearly black right after identification. It now starts at the current exposure and gain, tries the three white balances there, and steps exposure × gain by 2× (at most three times, never above the cap) only while the ball's own median brightness is outside 0.3–0.75. Gesture tracks used to be dropped after 0.25 s without a match (two missed frames at ~7.5 fps), and the next detection began a new track at 0 squares; they now coast for 1 s on half their velocity, the gate growing to cover a corner turned during the gap (`test_square_gesture_survives_missed_frames_at_live_rate`: every third detection missing, sometimes two in a row, one track, two squares). While searching, each moving track draws its trail; the leading one (most squares, then longest) is bright cyan and thick. Not yet tried live.

**Squares clip fixes (Nov 1, `clip_20261101_114812`, Fernando's squares not recognised).** Backlit evening room at 2 ms / gain 128 / 2800 K: the ball ~(35, 18, 8) of 255 against a near-black shirt and a bright wall, ~5 analysed fps, steps ~21 px per analysed frame (not too fast). Four causes, each fixed and replayed on the clip: (1) the refine rejected a ball joined to the dark arm (non-round region): it now takes the **largest inscribed circle** (distance transform) and requires the rim just outside it to be mostly background (≥55%); (2) a ring that is half shirt, half wall has a median neither has: ball pixels are now those **near the centre's colour** (within 0.7 of the distance to the ring's nearest quarter; 0.5 trimmed shaded rims, 0.6/0.7 swept); (3) **log-RGB features** (channel ratios, brightness weighted half) instead of Lab, which compresses dark colours (ball vs shirt ~18 → ~45 apart); ball proposed in 124 of 132 labelled frames (was 62), the far-ball clip still 47/50, radius ~8 px; (4) the square test needed the path back within a quarter of its size of its exact first point (real squares came back 99 px off a 58 px limit): it now counts a lap when the path **winds 360°** round its box's centre (lines and scribbles wind ~0); and a paused ball stopped being proposed and its one-square track was dropped: the leading tracks now get keep windows. Replayed through proposals → depth check → gesture, the clip **identifies the ball** at frame 2947 (`test_real_squares_clip_identifies_the_ball`, runs where the clip is in `throw_data`). Live searching is now ~180–200 ms per analysed frame (~5 fps) on the Pi 4. The scene was far too dark for throws anyway: more light on the throw area, not facing the ceiling lamp.

**Debug clips:** **● Record clip** / **■ Stop clip** saves every analysed frame (JPEG, the analysis's exact input), its decisions (`processed.jsonl`), depth at 5 fps, the annotated view at 5 fps and the settings, in any stage, to `~/omni_ws/bucket_sessions/clip_*` (90 s limit, ~4 MB/s). `bucket.sh clips` lists them, `bucket.sh fetch-clip [name]` copies one to `C:/Users/ferna/throw_data/bucket_clips/`.

**The key 3D test:** `flight3d.py` fits pixels and size/depth range jointly to `p'' = gravity − k |v| v`, with **gravity fixed by the bucket pitch**, not fitted to a random blob. It compares ordinary linear movement, measures acceleration independently in gravity's transverse 3D direction with propagated pixel/range uncertainty, and withholds the last two observations to test genuine forward prediction. Several causal suffix windows allow the ball to leave the hand; repeated successful checks confirm flight. Size-only motion remains tentative without independent depth support. Nearer foreground patches inconsistent with the known sphere size are rejected. Long gaps, wrong measured acceleration, weak observability, bad predictions or insufficient depth produce an explicit uncertain/rejected reason. These tests reduce false positives; a short held-ball movement can still mimic flight. The independent range is obtained from the **preceding** depth frame no older than 40 ms, using factory depth/colour extrinsics; no future depth or whole-clip association is used.

The costly fitting and trajectory integration have C++ implementations in `flight3d_native.cpp`, built on demand with the existing compiler into `~/.cache/omni_bucket`. The numpy reference is kept and cross-tested, with no scipy/ROS imports in these cores. This bench workflow uses the robot's native colour detector; it does **not** change the ROS catch nodes' default behaviour. It is an experimental gate to validate before adoption there.

The bucket forecast uses `throw_track/bucket.json`, colour/depth extrinsics, the ball radius and drag. It reports **likely in / likely out / rim or uncertain**, time to go and a provisional model band. It is not a calibrated probability or observed landing. The last forecast is labelled separately after tracking is lost; the current entry indicator clears. The camera pitch is adjustable, but this is not automatic calibration. A fixed-reference background check withdraws flight/prediction if the view shifts by >3 px; sparse scenes explicitly report an unverified guard. It does not correct ego-motion or replace a measured mount pose.

**Recording:** after Ready, every captured RGB frame is offered to a bounded asynchronous JPEG witness writer before the live processor skips to the newest frame. Decisions, sensor/host stamps, counters, exposure fields and decision age accompany it in `witness.jsonl`. Disk queue losses and processing skips are separate counters; capture counters expose earlier camera/USB losses. RGB is lossy JPEG, not raw sensor data. All rejected/uncertain candidates and manual **Mark attempt** actions can save a raw event clip (0.8 s before/after) from the full-rate colour/depth/IR rings, in the existing `tNNN/*.npy` format. Only one raw write runs at once; the Ready indicator accounts for raw writes/recording faults. **Raw depth/IR are not continuously archived between event clips.** A failed trigger still leaves the RGB witness, but full raw streams need a candidate or timely manual mark. This distinction matters for scoring misses.

Sessions go to `~/omni_ws/bucket_sessions/`, not Dropbox. Writers stop/decline arming below 1 GB free and never delete older data. Watch the recording-loss counter and wait while a raw clip is being written; the old SD-card pacing limit is still real. A graceful stop waits for pending raw writes. The optional `--arducam` witness (Nov 1; was a ~4 fps JPEG sidecar) runs the IMX708's full-field 2304×1296 mode (~56 fps), scaled by the ISP to 768×432, into a 2.4 s RAM ring (~130 MB); every raw event clip (candidate or **Mark attempt**) also gets its ±0.8 s as `tNNN/arducam.npy` + `arducam_stamps.npy` (sensor time mapped to the host wall clock, the D435's global_time domain, ±a few ms; host arrival; counter) + `arducam_info.npy` (exposure, gain). The page shows its fps and largest gap and a collapsible preview. Point it so it sees the flight's end and, if possible, the bucket rim: it is the only independent view of the outcome. It is **not calibrated/synchronized stereo**, rolling shutter (4 ms, gain 4, AWB) and does not contribute to the live 3D verdict. Concurrent D435 + Arducam CPU on the Pi 4 is unmeasured; if the D435's skip/loss counters climb, restart without `--arducam`.

## Run it

From the buttons in `Launcher.cmd` ("Cameras on the Pi": Ball view: Arducam / RealSense, Page, Stop view; its log is "ball_view" under Show log) or in `characterization/realsense/RealSense.cmd` (group 6), or from Git Bash in the repo:

```
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh picam        # Arducam / Camera Module 3
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh realsense    # RealSense D435
bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh synthetic    # a rendered ball, no camera
```

It copies the detector and this folder to `~/omni_ws` on the Pi, starts the server there, opens `http://omni-cam.local:8085/` and stays in the foreground (Ctrl-C stops it; `bv.sh stop` if one is left running). One process holds one camera, so run one view at a time. Arguments after the source go to `ball_view.py`: `--mode wide --lens wide` (picam), `--size 640x480 --fps 60` (realsense), `--numpy` (the numpy detector), `--port`. `bv.sh test` runs the tests on the PC; `ball_view.py --source synthetic` also runs on the PC alone.

The Pi's `~/.venvs/cam` has everything (Picamera2, `pyrealsense2` 2.59.1, Pillow, simplejpeg, numpy); the C++ detector builds on first use with g++.

## What the page shows

- **The image** with every candidate blob circled at its measured radius (green: the one the detector reports; grey: other candidates; orange: candidates that haven't moved), the tracking window (cyan), the track (orange line), and a one-line readout. Views: colour, or the depth map (RealSense).
- **Tracking:** capture fps, detection time (median / p95), capture → detection age, the share of the last 120 frames with the ball, the **centroid noise** (the second differences of a smooth track: the measurement noise in px the tracker would see), the blob's centre, radius and pixel count.
- **Position (camera frame, m):** from the ball's size on both cameras, from the depth map on the RealSense, with the depth fill and noise, the size-vs-depth disagreement and the size gain that would make them agree.
- **Knobs:** hue window, saturation, value, minimum blob size, size gain, coarse stride; the ball's diameter and the focal scale; exposure, gain and white balance. Click the ball to set the colour window from it (hue ±20°, saturation and value at 60% of the clicked pixel's). "Tint" paints the pixels that pass the colour test, which is the quickest way to see why a blob is or isn't found.
- **The video stays in view** while you scroll the controls (the page's window is wide enough for two columns, 980 px or more; the picture is sized to fit the window's height).
- **What the colour filter accepts** (under the colour sliders), and how to set it by drawing: a **rainbow** (hue across, saturation up) with the window as the bright rectangle and the rest dimmed. Drag a new rectangle on it: its left and right sides are hue min / max and its bottom is the saturation floor (the detector has no upper saturation limit, so the top is always 1); a plain click picks a hue ±3°; drag a side (within 7 px) to move just that side, or the bottom edge to move the floor. Below it, a **saturation × value square** at the window's middle hue: click or drag the dullest, darkest colour to accept (the bright part passes). The sliders follow what you draw and still work. Swatches show the dullest and most vivid colours at the window's two edges. **Pink** marks where the detected blob's own pixels fall (5th–95th percentile of hue, saturation, value; the white tick is the median) with a swatch of its mean colour: if a pink mark sticks out of the bright part, the filter is losing part of the ball.
- **Size markers in the picture** ("show the aiming ring and the size legend"): a dotted cyan **ring** at the image centre, the size the ball has at the 'known distance' knob (hold the ball in it), and a **legend** of true-scale circles in the bottom-left: the minimum blob (pink, with the distance beyond which a ball is smaller than it), the ball at the known distance (cyan) and the detected blob (green; circles over 16 px are capped, the label says so). The "beyond ~N m" figure uses the larger of the minimum blob's radius and half the coarse stride: measured on clean discs, the stride-4 whole-frame pass finds r 2 px 80% of the time and r 2.5 px always, below the 8 px minimum blob's own 1.6 px; a real ball's dim rim does worse. "Ring the blobs rejected as too small" circles in red what the minimum size throws away (it costs one more detector pass per frame).
- **Auto-tune:** *auto-tune on the ring* takes the pixels in the middle three quarters of the cyan ring (hold the ball in it; set 'known distance' to how far away it is, or the ring is the wrong size), finds their dominant colour (so a little background doesn't widen the window), and sets hue min / max (2nd–98th percentile of the ball's hue ± 8°), saturation and value floors (0.85 × the 5th percentile) and the minimum blob (a quarter of the ball's pixel count: it is still found at about twice the distance). *On the detected blob* does the same on the green blob. It checks the result on that frame (the ball must come out as a blob at the target), says in the message how many *other* blobs of that colour still pass elsewhere in the image (a yellow shirt would), and **undo** restores the five knobs. It doesn't touch size gain, focal scale or stride. A red ball can't be tuned: the detector's hue window doesn't wrap through 0° (the message says so).
- **Save settings** (Settings section): writes the chosen ball-tracker parameters to the Pi as `~/omni_ws/ball_view_settings/<source>.json` (`picam` or `realsense`: the latest) plus a dated copy, and downloads the same file in the browser. The file has `detector` (hue_deg, min_sat, min_val, min_blob_px, size_gain, coarse_stride: a `DetectorConfig`'s fields, ready for the robot's config), `ball_mm`, `focal_scale`, `camera` (exposure, gain, white balance), `rules` (track, ignore_still), the image size and focal length it was made with, and `knobs` (every slider and tick box, for loading back). The next view of that camera **starts from its saved file** (`--fresh` ignores it, the page's *reset* goes back to the defaults), and *load saved* reapplies it. It lives outside `~/omni_ws/ball_view/` because a deploy replaces that folder. `bash characterization/ball_view/bv.sh fetch` (Launcher: "Get saved settings") copies the Pi's files to `characterization/ball_view/settings/` in the repo.
- **Rules to compare:** *acquisition* is the whole frame and the largest ball-coloured blob wins, the weakness `detector.py` documents (a yellow shirt hides the ball). Tick "acquisition ignores stationary blobs" for the robot's rule: its tracker starts on the blob that moves (a blob within 1 px of where one was in the last frame is rejected). Tick "track in a window" for the mode after the first lock: only a window around the predicted position is searched, and a lost track goes back to acquisition. Changing either starts over.

## How the 3D position is made

| | From | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **size** (both cameras) | `z = f R / b`: f the focal length in px, R the ball's radius, b the blob's measured radius (the minor semi-axis of its moments, what the robot measures); x, y from the pixel and f | f. On the Pi camera it comes from the lens (4.74 mm standard, 2.75 mm wide, 1.4 µm pixels) and the sensor crop libcamera reports for the frame; on the D435 from the camera's calibration. If you don't trust it, put the ball at a known distance, enter it, press **calibrate focal**: it sets the focal scale. The 120 fps mode crops the sensor to 2/3 of its width: the page reports f = 846 px, a 48.6° field of view, at 768 px wide |
| **depth** (RealSense) | the median depth over the inner 60% of the ball's disc, plus the ball's radius (depth sees the front surface); x, y by pinhole at that depth | the depth map aligned to the colour image (`rs.align`), so the ball is found in colour and measured in depth |

The size estimate's error is shown as the range change for half a pixel of blob radius, `z · 0.5 / b`: with the Pi camera's 846 px focal length the ball's radius is b = 31 px at 1 m (1.6 cm per half pixel), 10 px at 3 m (16 cm) and 4.8 px at 6.5 m (68 cm). So size alone is a bench check and a close-range estimate; at throw distance the robot triangulates a stereo pair. The D435's depth is for 0.3-3 m, not the throw distance.

**Calibrating `size_gain`:** the colour threshold drops the ball's dim rim, so a blob reads small (`detector.py`: "calibrate on the bench"). On the RealSense, with the ball at a distance where the depth fills (0.5-2 m), the page shows "size gain that agrees": the gain at which the size range equals the depth range. Hold the ball at several distances; a value that stays put is the number to set (button "apply suggested size gain").

## Not tested

Nothing here was run with a real ball in view (the Oct 30 session had none). What ran on the Pi, over HTTP from the PC: the Arducam at 120 fps with detection (1.7 ms median) and the exposure knob (500 µs goes near black, as it should); the D435's colour image with the depth aligned to it at 30 fps, with a depth reading at a detected blob (a false one: a label on a bottle); the MJPEG stream at 18 fps over Wi-Fi. The swatches, ring, legend and auto-tune were added later (Oct 30): checked on the synthetic ball (the page in headless Chrome, `test_ball_view.py`) and on the Arducam with no ball in view (status, ring, legend, and auto-tune's "no colour in the ring" answer); auto-tune on a real ball hasn't been tried, so how well the 0.85 floors and the ±8° margin suit your lights and ball is open. Drawing on the rainbow and in the square, the sticky video and the Save button were driven with real pointer events in Chrome (Playwright, synthetic source); Save, fetch and load were run against the Arducam view on the Pi. Not exercised on the hardware: the colour click, the D435's exposure and white-balance knobs, the Arducam's white-balance knob, "calibrate focal", and any clicking or dragging in the page (it was loaded once in headless Chrome against the synthetic source: the sliders built, the readouts filled and updated; the endpoints behind the controls were called directly). The pipeline end to end on the synthetic ball is in `test_ball_view.py`: detection within 0.6 px of the truth, size range within 4%, depth within 1%. The size / depth agreement on the real ball, the hue window under your lights and the centroid noise are what to read off first.
